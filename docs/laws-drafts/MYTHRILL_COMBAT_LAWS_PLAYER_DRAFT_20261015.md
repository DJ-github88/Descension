# Combat: A Player's Reference (Draft)

> **Draft for creator review. Not published; it does not replace any Laws page.**
> Built from the rev3 combat proposal (2026-10-14) and the creator's approved decisions.
> Unmarked lines are approved decisions or current Laws. **[Proposed]** = recommended wording that still needs approval. **[Open]** = not decided; the GM rules at the table until it is.

> **Playing in the VTT today.** The VTT does not yet run reaction opportunities, armor rolls, armor wear or elixir use for you. Play them the way this page describes for the table: say what you do, roll on screen or with your own dice, and the GM enters the final result. Change an item's DR die on your sheet yourself (or ask the GM) when it wears.

---

## 1. Your first fight

**Initiative.** Everyone rolls once: 1d20 + your Agility modifier. Highest goes first. Ties go to the higher Agility score.

**Choose how you block.** Pick your *favored* armor piece or shield, the one you angle into blows. You can list backups in order, or choose "Don't block". You set this on your turn and can change it on any of your turns.

**Spend AP on your turn.** Moving costs 1 AP. Spells and abilities list their own cost. Anything you don't spend is what you react with later.

**Attack.** Roll your weapon die. A natural 1 misses. The top face is a critical: roll again and add. Anything else hits for the face plus your Strength or Agility modifier, as your weapon says.

**When you're hit.** Roll your blocking piece's DR die and subtract it from the damage. Then look at the face you rolled: a low face wears the piece down (section 3).

**Off your turn.** When something happens that you can react to, you have eight seconds to say so. You never have to answer.

**Elixirs.** Drinking one costs 0 AP, and you can do it at any time, even on someone else's turn.

**At the table:** say your favored piece out loud at the start of the fight, keep your AP visible, and call reactions before the GM moves on.

## 2. Spending AP, and saving for reactions

Combat is built around **four AP**. *[Open: how much AP you have each turn, how it refills, any race differences, and what a weapon strike costs. Current Laws pages still say 3 AP and a 2 AP attack; those numbers are under review.]*

- **On your turn,** spend AP in any order: move, attack, cast, move again.
- **Temporary AP and mana** count toward what you can afford and are spent first. A 4-mana spell can be paid with 3 temporary mana and 1 ordinary.
- **Saving AP.** AP left at the end of your turn is what you use for reactions until your next turn starts. You can't spend AP you don't have. *[Open: what happens to unspent AP when your next turn starts depends on the refill rule above.]*
- **Saving other resources.** Reactions also spend any mana or class resource their card lists, so keep enough back for the reaction you're planning.
- **Elixirs need no AP,** so you never have to save AP for one.

**Rule of thumb:** before you end your turn, ask "what will I want to do when they hit back?" Parry and Riposte each cost 1 AP.

## 3. Blocking, DR, Defend, wear and Evasion

### Your favored blocking piece
Your armor protects you through its **equipment DR die**. When a blow lands, you roll the die of the piece that takes it and subtract the result. You don't choose a piece on every hit. Your favorite takes it, and you only roll.

If your favorite can't block (it broke or is no longer worn), your first available backup blocks instead.

### Equipment DR versus flat DR
- **Equipment DR die:** the die on your armor or shield. You roll it when hit, and it wears.
- **Flat DR:** a rare, fixed number from special gear or a powerful spell, such as Aegis Ward's +3. It is subtracted without rolling and never wears.

There is no Armor Score and no soak die.

### Defend (1 AP, on your turn)
Brace for what's coming. Until your next turn, your blocking piece's DR rolls **count double** and **never wear it**. Defend is an action you take on your turn, not a reaction.

### Wear: read the face, not the total
- **Armor** wears one die size when its DR die shows **1 or 2**.
- **Shields** wear only on a **1**.
- Wear always reads the number on the die, never the doubled total. A braced vest rolling 2 counts as 4 and doesn't wear.
- Worn below d4, a piece **breaks**: it comes off and its enchantments stop.

### Special cases
- A **critical hit** goes around armor: no DR roll and no wear. *[Open: whether criticals also go around shields.]*
- **Riposte** ignores flat DR and the doubling from Defend. The target's equipment DR die still rolls as normal.
- The **piercing critical** that lowers DR by 2 lowers flat DR only. It never shrinks an equipment DR die.
- **Raise Shield:** *[Open. Its current text ("durability decreases by 1") conflicts with the shield-wear rule above, and new wording is under review. Until then, the GM rules on how it wears your shield.]*
- *[Open: whether your equipment DR die reduces non-physical damage, and the order in which resistances, DR and flat DR apply.]*

### Evasion: does the attack land?
Evasion decides **whether** a hit lands. DR decides **how much** a hit that lands hurts.

An attack misses on a natural 1, and each **+1 Evasion** adds one more missing face. Against a d6 attack: with no Evasion only a 1 misses; with +1, a 1 or 2 misses; with +2, 1 to 3 miss.

## 4. Reacting off your turn

### Eight seconds to decide
Each reaction names its moment: when someone is targeted, when an attack roll succeeds, when you're hit, or after damage. You can use a reaction only at its own moment, and only if you can pay for it then.

When that moment comes and you hold a reaction that fits, you get **eight seconds to declare it**.
- The eight seconds are for **deciding**, not rolling. Dice are rolled afterwards, at your own pace.
- You don't have to answer. Saying nothing means no reaction. *[Proposed: an optional "Pass" lets the fight move on sooner.]*
- If nobody holds a reaction that fits, nothing opens and play just continues.
- The countdown never lets you cast an ordinary spell outside your turn.
- The GM can pause or extend the countdown.

*[Open: whether you can react to something an enemy does during your own turn, such as an Opportunity Attack as you move past it.]*

### Hidden declarations
What you declare stays hidden from the other players until the reactions resolve. Only you and the GM know. Several people may react to the same moment. *[Proposed: they resolve in a fixed order, never by who was fastest: first anything that stops the action, then redirects, then avoiding, then absorbing.]*

A reaction that happens pays its full price, even if something else turned out to make it unnecessary. *[Open: what you pay if you declared a reaction but it never happened, for example because an ally's counterspell stopped the spell first.]*

### Follow-ups: Parry → Riposte
Some reactions unlock a **follow-up** that is written on a card: Riposte after *your* successful Parry, Shield Bash after *your* successful Raise Shield. When yours succeeds, you get a fresh eight-second opportunity to use the follow-up, if you still have the AP and are in reach (Riposte: 5 ft).

A follow-up belongs to the character whose reaction succeeded. If a creature parries you, you gain nothing from it. The creature gets a Riposte only if it has one.

*[Open: the limits that stop reactions answering each other forever. They will never block a follow-up a card names, such as Riposte after Parry.]*

### At the table
There is no timer. Call your reaction before the GM moves on to the next step, and the GM may count down aloud. *[Proposed: to keep declarations hidden, everyone who wants to react shows their choice at the same time on the GM's count, by laying down or pointing to the card, or by writing it down.]*

## 5. Elixirs

- Drinking an elixir costs **0 AP**, and you can do it **at any time**: on your turn, on someone else's turn, or while a reaction countdown is running. This is an exception to the 1 AP "use an item" action. Other items keep their listed cost.
- Each drink uses one elixir. Its quantity, charges and any restrictions written on it still apply.
- An elixir works from the moment you drink it. It never changes something that has already happened.

*[Open: exactly how late in an attack an elixir still counts for that attack, for example a healing elixir drunk after the roll but before the damage lands.]*

**At the table, and in the VTT today:** say "I drink my healing elixir", remove one from your inventory yourself, and the GM applies its effect.

## 6. Worked examples

*Hypothetical characters. Each example reads the same at a physical table and in the VTT.*

### A. A goblin, Evasion and a worn vest
Ilsa has **Evasion +1** and favors her **vest (DR d10)**. A goblin swings a d6 sword (+1).
1. The goblin rolls **2**. With Evasion +1, a 1 or 2 misses. **Missed.**
2. Next swing: **5**. That hits, for 5 + 1 = **6**.
3. Ilsa rolls her vest's d10: **2**. She takes 6 − 2 = **4**. The face was 2, so the vest wears from d10 to **d8**.

*If she had braced with Defend:* the 2 counts as 4, she takes 2, and the vest doesn't wear.
*If her shield (d8) had blocked and rolled 2:* she takes 4, and the shield holds, because shields wear only on a 1.

### B. Parry, then Riposte
A bandit stands next to Ilsa and swings a d8 longsword (+3). Ilsa kept **2 AP**. Her shortsword is a d6, and her modifier is +2.
1. The bandit rolls **4**: a hit for 7.
2. **Reaction moment.** Ilsa holds Parry (1 AP). She declares it within the eight seconds. At a table, she calls "Parry!" before the GM moves on.
3. Ilsa rolls her d6: **6**, which explodes. She rolls again, gets **4** and adds it: **10**. That beats the bandit's roll, so the strike is turned aside.
4. **Follow-up.** Her Parry succeeded, the bandit is within 5 ft, and she has 1 AP left, so Riposte is offered to her alone, with a fresh eight seconds. She declares it.
5. She rolls her d6: **3**, + 2 = **5** damage. The bandit wears no armor. If it did, its equipment DR die would still roll, but any flat DR or Defend doubling would not count.

Ilsa spent both AP.

### C. An elixir on someone else's turn
Ana, an unarmored mage, spent all her AP on her turn. On Branvig's turn, a Feather Slash leaves her at **4 HP**.
1. Before Branvig's next attack, Ana drinks a healing elixir. It costs **0 AP**, so having none left doesn't matter. It restores 8 (an example amount), so she is now at **12 HP**, with one elixir left.
2. Branvig's next Feather Slash deals **11**. Ana has no armor to roll. She drops to **1 HP**, still standing.

At the table and in the VTT today: Ana says she drinks, removes the elixir from her inventory, and the GM adds the healing.

## 7. Creator decisions needed before publication
*Not part of the player draft.*

1. **AP:** AP per turn, how it refills, race differences, and weapon strike cost (J3). Current Laws pages still say 3 AP and a 2 AP attack.
2. **Unexecuted reactions:** what a declared reaction costs if it never happens (J4).
3. **Own-turn reactions:** whether an enemy's trigger during your own turn opens a reaction opportunity (J5).
4. **Raise Shield:** new wording to replace "durability −1" so it fits face-1 shield wear (rev3 offers R1/R2/R3).
5. **Evasion:** the cap or top face, stacking, whether the extra missing faces count as fumbles, formula attacks and spells without an attack roll, and how Dodge, Evade and Apex Evasion convert.
6. **Damage order:** the mitigation order, including conversion, and whether equipment DR reduces non-physical damage.
7. **Several reactions at once:** the resolution order, the optional Pass, and continuing early once everyone has declared (all [Proposed]).
8. **Follow-up and loop wording:** the safety limit text. It must keep card-named follow-ups such as Parry → Riposte and Raise Shield → Shield Bash. Rev3's "nobody reacts to their own reaction" is withdrawn here because it contradicts Riposte, and its four-window fallback is a VTT automation idea, not a table rule.
9. **Hidden declarations at a physical table:** confirm the simultaneous-reveal procedure, or name another.
10. **Elixir timing in the middle of an attack** (rev3's proposed cutoffs).
11. **Smaller gaps the text touches:** whether criticals also go around shields, and whether Parry compares the attacker's die face or their total.
