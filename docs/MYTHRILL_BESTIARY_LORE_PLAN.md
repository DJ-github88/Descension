# MYTHRILL BESTIARY LORE PLAN

**Status:** Source-of-truth planning draft; proposed canon, not yet merged into the canonical framework or runtime data.

**Purpose:** Rebuild the Mythrill bestiary as a deliberate worldbuilding layer. Each creature must be a real inhabitant, spirit, lineage, construct, death-form, or later invader with a specific place in Mythrill rather than a generic monster produced by a universal rule.

**Scope:** This document audits and gives a working lore treatment to every record in `vtt-react/public/data/creatures.json` in its existing file order. It also reconciles the bestiary against `CORE_LORE_FRAMEWORK.md`, `vtt-react/public/data/rules.json`, `vtt-react/public/data/lore.json`, and `Nordhalla_Complete_Lore.md`. It does not change any of those files.

**Working count:** 193 creature records, numbered 001-193 below.

## 1. Canon Decisions

These decisions control this draft. Where the inspected files disagree, this document labels the conflict instead of silently preserving it.

1. **Mythrill was already inhabited.** Before the Great Binding, Mythrill had native beasts, spirits, creature-kinds, and mythic peoples. They were dangerous, intelligent, ecological, and culturally meaningful without Keth-Amar, the Wyrd, or mortal magic.
2. **Native origin is independent of the Wyrd.** A native creature does not exist because mortals told a story about it. Folklore records, misnames, negotiates with, fears, or misunderstands a creature that already exists.
3. **The Old World had no mortal magic.** Native beings may be intrinsically supernatural, spiritual, divine-adjacent, elemental, or mythic. That is not the same as mortal spellcasting. Any creature that uses a later mortal magic should be marked as an adaptation, pact, tool, or later corruption.
4. **Aethil and Aex contacted the houses.** They communicated with the mortal houses and introduced celestial magic through the blood oath so the houses could protect Sol. The oath changed house lineages and lands; it did not create the pre-existing bestiary.
5. **The First Cracks came before Keth-Amar.** Oath failures, lineage breaks, and other house fractures damaged Aex's shell before Keth-Amar arrived. Sol warmth leaked into regions, creating thermal pockets and changing migration, breeding, dormancy, aggression, and habitat boundaries for native life.
6. **Keth-Amar is later, but the Wyrd ecology is older.** Keth-Amar arrives after the First Cracks/Warmth era and brings, directs, attracts, or exploits the Wyrd as a distinct cosmic force. Ancient Cosmic Wyrdkin already exist beyond Mythrill; the Wyrd is not the source of Mythrill's native bestiary.
7. **Wyrd layers are distinct.** Ancient Cosmic Wyrdkin are pre-existing extra-Mythrill organisms or entities. Keth-spawn/Wyrdspawn are narrower entities directly generated by Keth-Amar's Wyrd after arrival. A native individual or branch can be Wyrd-touched without the entire species becoming Wyrdspawn.
8. **Old God service is optional.** A creature may serve, bargain with, descend from, or oppose an Old God or native deity, but no such relationship is assumed. Each entry must first answer what the creature itself is and what it does.
9. **Agency is required.** Even predators have a pattern of choice, territory, instinct, social order, or ecological pressure. A monster is not reduced to a moral label or a combat role.
10. **Folklore is evidence, not genesis.** A folk tale can preserve a true encounter, encode a survival practice, distort a political memory, or describe a Wyrdspawn's tell. It cannot create a native creature.
11. **No universal creature conversion.** Some individuals, broods, factions, or lineages may later be altered by warmth, house magic, Aex Shards, or Wyrd exposure. That is a deliberate per-creature decision, not a rule applied to all members of a kind.
12. **Working names are provisional.** References to a patron, native deity, Old God, court, or ancestral title are working labels only. This plan does not finalize a pantheon name for every entry.

### Data-audit rules

- Keep the exact JSON `id` and display `name` in every inventory heading, even when the display value is obviously wrong.
- Treat `type`, `tags`, and legacy `origin` prose as data to reconcile, not as automatic canon.
- When a current field is copied from another creature, say so in **Next decision** and use `DATA-CORRECTION` rather than laundering the text into a final origin.
- A native entry may retain a later Wyrd relationship, but that relationship must describe an individual, branch, outbreak, or post-Keth variant unless the full kind is intentionally re-authored as invasive.

## 3. Status Vocabulary

- **NATIVE-PROPOSED:** The record already supports an independent pre-Binding creature-kind or individual. Minor wording cleanup may remain.
- **NATIVE-REVIEW:** The creature is probably native, but its maker, death-state, patron relationship, or timeline needs a focused lore decision.
- **COSMIC-WYRD-PROPOSED:** Ancient extra-Mythrill Wyrdkin is the leading origin; a direct Keth relationship may be strategic or unknown rather than parental.
- **COSMIC-WYRD-REVIEW:** The record may be ancient cosmic Wyrdkin, a direct Keth-spawn, or a local cosmic colony and needs provenance, anchor, or dependency evidence.
- **WYRDSPAWN-PROPOSED:** The record explicitly or provisionally describes a direct Keth-generated offspring, larva, breach body, or local manifestation whose continued existence depends on Keth's Wyrd.
- **HYBRID-REVIEW:** The record contains a credible native baseline plus a later Wyrd alteration, or its present form cannot yet be separated into native and Wyrdspawn layers.
- **DATA-CORRECTION:** The record has a direct copy, name, field, or identity error. The working classification after correction is stated in the heading, but the current record is not final evidence.
- **POST-BINDING-REVIEW:** The current origin implies a mortal-made or house-era creation rather than a pre-Binding native kind. It must be retained only if the setting wants a non-native constructed category alongside natives and Wyrdspawn.

## 4. Working Timeline

The sequence below is canon for this plan. The dates use the existing 475-year framework as a working index only; the eight-century language in Nordhalla and other lore requires a later calendar decision.

### Old World - before Year 0

Mythrill is dark, dangerous, and inhabited. Native animals, river guardians, mountain spirits, death-forms, fey courts, elemental presences, mythic peoples, and old constructs occupy territories without mortal magic. They have food webs, migration routes, taboos, rivalries, trade, stewardship, predation, and their own histories. Some are associated with native deities or Old Gods; many are not.

Folklore begins here as a mortal response to contact. A story about a creature can be accurate, partial, political, or wrong. It does not summon the creature into existence.

### Year 0 - the Great Binding

Aethil and Aex communicate with the houses and plead for help protecting Sol, the unhatched celestial infant hidden within Mythrill. The houses swear blood oaths to Aex's shell. Celestial magic enters mortal lineages through those oaths, producing later house gifts, relics, curses, and subraces. The native bestiary predates this event and remains independent of the new mortal magic.

The Binding changes the world around existing life: darkness, cold, altered coastlines, glacier pressure, closed passes, and new political borders force creatures to move, hibernate, adapt, guard new resources, or contest house settlements.

### Years 0-300 - First Cracks and Sol Warmth

House oath failures, lineage breaks, purges, failed inheritances, and other fractures damage Aex's shell before Keth-Amar's arrival. Sol warmth vents into regions. The warmth does not create creatures; it changes habitats and behavior. Warm bogs, steam corridors, volcanic rivers, thawed caves, thermal forests, and navigable water pockets become new ecological pressures.

This is the period in which a creature's present-day range may diverge from its Old World range. Any entry that says a creature was "born from" a warm vent should first be tested as an older kind responding to that vent.

### Year 300 - Keth-Amar's arrival

Keth-Amar follows the Astril trail and reaches Mythrill after the native world and the house-warmth changes already exist. Keth-Amar studies house fears, bargains, native folklore, and regional vulnerabilities. The Wyrd enters as Keth-Amar's distinct corruptive pressure; it is not retroactively the cause of the Old World creatures.

### Years 300-325 - infiltration and preparation for the Breach

Keth-Amar uses the Wyrd to infiltrate mortal memory, imitate trusted voices, and prepare invasive manifestations. A native creature can be targeted or altered during this period, but its kind remains a native kind unless a separate Wyrdspawn lineage is deliberately established.

### Year 325 - the Breach / Blizzard's End

Keth-Amar manipulates house heirs and the seal fails catastrophically but incompletely. Aex fractures, Sol warmth erupts through new vents, and Aex Shards scatter. The Wyrd becomes an enduring environmental and metaphysical threat. This is the first point at which a creature can be straightforwardly authored as a post-Keth Wyrdspawn without contradicting the Old World.

### Year 475 - present-day working index

The present is a freezing era of partial warmth, Aex Shards, house politics, old native territories, and invasive Wyrdspawn outbreaks. Native creatures have agency in the present: they may resist, exploit, bargain with, hunt, shelter from, or become individually affected by the Wyrd. The exact elapsed-years label remains open because Nordhalla's complete lore uses an eight-century scale.

## 5. Creature-Origin Taxonomy

Use one primary origin and, only where necessary, one later modifier.

1. **Primordial native life:** A beast, predator, herd, scavenger, or symbiotic lifeform that evolved or emerged from Mythrill's pre-Binding land, sea, climate, or deep earth. No divine maker is required.
2. **Native spirit or land-person:** A river, forest, wind, hearth, mountain, storm, spring, threshold, or seasonal being with its own agency and territory. It may be called a god by mortals without being a created servant.
3. **Native mythic people or lineage:** A creature-kind with language, social memory, customs, inheritance, territory, and internal politics. It must not be reduced to a single folklore trait.
4. **Native death, ancestor, or liminal form:** A pre-Binding relationship between life, death, burial, memory, and place. The author must explain the rule that permits the form to persist; it is not automatically Wyrd or mortal necromancy.
5. **Native construct or sacred work:** A pre-Binding object, statue, vessel, tool, or guardian that was made by a named culture, craft tradition, or optional deity. The maker and purpose must be specific. "An Old God made it" is not a sufficient answer.
6. **House-era or post-Binding construct:** A later mortal-made being that is not native in the strict sense and is not Wyrdspawn. Use `POST-BINDING-REVIEW` until its place in the setting is wanted.
7. **Native kind with later Wyrd exposure:** The baseline remains native. A branch, individual, or outbreak can be `HYBRID-REVIEW` and should retain a recognizable native motive that the Wyrd distorts.
8. **Wyrdspawn:** A post-Keth invasive entity whose continued existence depends on Wyrd occupation, Wyrd assembly, or a Wyrd breach. It may borrow a native body or a cultural shape, but its origin is the later invasion.

## 6. Per-Creature Authoring Template

Every record below uses this template in compact form. These are working answers, not locked prose. Native records keep the native fields; cosmic Wyrd records add the cosmic fields explicitly.

- **True origin:** What the creature was before arriving on Mythrill, whether it is native, ancient cosmic Wyrdkin, direct Keth-spawn, Wyrd-touched native, spirit, people, individual, construct, or later invader. State a maker only when useful and verified.
- **Cosmic provenance:** For a Wyrd creature, identify its working-name habitat in the dark between stars, relationship to dying stars and Keth-Amar's hunting ecology, and whether it is fauna, symbiote, servant, scout, parasite, or predator. For a native, write "not applicable; native Mythrill origin."
- **Agency / ecological-world function:** What it chooses, needs, teaches, hunts, maintains, pollinates, carries, predicts, trades, listens for, drains, or disrupts. Give even hostile creatures a pattern beyond "attacks."
- **Habitat / territory:** The exact region, subregion, route, water, cave, grove, settlement edge, or seasonal range it claims.
- **Values / what it guards:** The thing that matters to it: offspring, a river, a contract, a memory, a tomb, a migration, a craft, a boundary, an anchor, or its own freedom.
- **Folklore truth:** What local people get right, what they misread, and what survival practice or political story the folklore preserves. After arrival, folklore may camouflage, translate, or tactically blueprint a Wyrd creature; it does not create the native creature or the cosmic species.
- **Binding / warmth effect:** How the Great Binding, First Cracks, Sol warmth, Aex-shell damage, or the Breach changed its territory or behavior. "No direct effect" is a valid answer.
- **Wyrd function / role:** The strategic or ecological role it performs, if any: scout/listener, anchor/breach-keeper, drainer/harvester, parasite/sower, mimic/interpreter, siege organism, collector/judge, or independent cosmic predator.
- **Mythrill anchor / nest:** The crack, Aex Shard, infected matter, Wyrd breach, wake-trail, glacier, root, ash vent, wreck, cave, hearth, or infrastructure that lets it arrive or persist.
- **Keth-Amar relationship:** Directly generated, directed, attracted by his wake, exploited as a tool, preyed upon, symbiotic with his hunt, independent, or unknown. Do not assume command or parentage.
- **Current independence:** Whether it remains an active Keth instrument, follows an inherited instinct, has formed a stranded colony or faction, negotiates with locals, or is now independent after Keth-Amar's retreat.
- **Wyrd relationship / status:** Native baseline, ancient cosmic Wyrdkin, direct Keth-spawn, Wyrd-touched native, resistant, opportunistic, or unresolved. A Wyrd-touched native is not a new species.
- **Countermeasure:** The ecological, social, linguistic, ritual, navigational, or combat response that addresses the creature's actual dependency or purpose. Killing a body alone may be temporary.
- **Next decision:** The smallest lore decision needed before final prose, taxonomy, or data can be locked.

## 7. Layered Wyrd Ecology

The Wyrd is not a synonym for every supernatural creature in Mythrill. It is a cosmic medium and corruptive force that reaches Mythrill after Keth-Amar's arrival. It carries an older ecology with it, and it can also generate new local offspring or alter native individuals. These layers must remain distinct.

### 7.1 Layer Definitions

1. **The Wyrd - cosmic force/medium/corruption:** A hostile, reality-warping medium associated with Keth-Amar's hunger, the dark between stars, and the routes by which that hunger crosses space. It can carry organisms, alter matter, connect minds, and sustain breaches. It is not itself a species and does not automatically create whatever local people fear.
2. **Ancient Cosmic Wyrdkin / Wyrd creatures:** Pre-existing organisms, entities, colonies, and ecological forms from beyond Mythrill. They are older than their arrival here and need not be generated by Keth-Amar. They may be fauna, symbiotes, scouts, parasites, collectors, servants, or independent predators of the cosmic dark. Their working habitat is the **Between**, a provisional name for the dark between stars; the final cosmic geography remains open.
3. **Keth-spawn / Wyrdspawn:** Entities, larvae, bodies, or local manifestations directly generated by Keth-Amar's Wyrd after it reaches Mythrill. This includes a Wyrd condensation, a breach-born offspring, a Wyrd body assembled from local matter, or a local form whose continued existence depends on Keth's direct corruptive output. Reserve `WYRDSPAWN-PROPOSED` for this narrower category.
4. **Wyrd-touched native:** A native Mythrill creature, individual, brood, or branch that has been infected, altered, coerced, or symbiotically changed by Wyrd exposure. The native population remains native. A Wyrd-touched condition is not a new species and must be authored as an individual, branch, outbreak, or reversible/irreversible state.

Ancient Cosmic Wyrdkin can be dangerous without being Keth's offspring. Keth-Amar can direct, attract, trade with, starve, weaponize, or simply exploit them as they follow his wake. Some recognize him as a predator, some as a route-maker, some as a source of food, and some as an opportunity. The relationship is a per-kind or per-faction decision.

### 7.2 Cosmic Origin and Persistence

The dark between stars is a working habitat, not a finalized cosmological name. It contains cold voids, dying-star debris, abandoned thermal shells, pressure currents, and Wyrd ecologies that live by sensing the life, heat, memory, or structural resonance of celestial bodies. Some Wyrd creatures feed on dying stars; some shelter in their debris; some migrate along predator wakes; some are born from or reproduce through Wyrd medium; and some are independent animals that Keth-Amar encounters during his hunts.

Keth-Amar's relationship to Sol creates a route rather than a universal origin. The Astril trail and Keth-Amar's wake can attract cosmic fauna. Aex-shell cracks can expose Mythrill to organisms already traveling in that pressure. Wyrd breaches can function as doors or nurseries. Infected matter can carry a larva, spore, egg, colony, or dormant body. Aex Shards can act as resonant anchors, beacons, food, cages, or territorial objects. None of these routes means that local rumor created the creature.

Once on Mythrill, a Wyrd creature needs a deliberate persistence condition. It may anchor in a glacier, root system, ash vent, wreck, hearth, cave, mineral vein, contract archive, pipe network, corpse field, or Shard site. It may feed on warmth, memory, pressure, grief, blood, contract energy, living cycles, or Wyrd itself. If Keth-Amar is pushed back, the creature may remain because the nest is self-sustaining, because it has reproduced, because it has nowhere to return, because it has chosen independence, or because the anchor is still active.

Propagation is likewise varied. A cosmic Wyrd species may lay eggs, bud colonies, divide through infected matter, exchange symbiotes, reproduce in a breach, consume and copy hosts, or remain a solitary long-lived predator. Direct Keth-spawn may be sterile, short-lived, or dependent on continuing Wyrd output. Intelligence follows ecology: some creatures are simple hunters, some use signal and territory, some possess language, some form factions, and some can negotiate even when their goals are dangerous.

### 7.3 Wyrd Role Taxonomy

These are functions, not moral alignments and not mandatory species classes. One kind may perform several roles, and a role can change after Keth-Amar retreats.

- **Scouts / listeners:** Sense Sol warmth, Aex-shell stress, house bloodlines, Astril resonance, or native resistance. They may report to Keth-Amar, to a colony, or only to themselves.
- **Anchors / breach-keepers:** Hold open a crack, stabilize a Wyrd route, guard an Aex Shard, or maintain a nest through which other entities can pass.
- **Drainers / harvesters:** Feed on heat, memory, blood, contract force, life cycles, or the energy leaking from a binding.
- **Parasites / sowers:** Enter hosts, hearths, roots, pipes, corpses, ships, or soil and spread a later outbreak or biological stage.
- **Mimics / interpreters:** Translate cosmic signals into local bodies, stolen voices, familiar faces, contracts, songs, or regional shapes. They camouflage an arrival; they do not prove that local folklore created it.
- **Siege organisms:** Attack infrastructure, sanctuaries, migration routes, ports, mines, cities, or warmth sources as a sustained strategic pressure.
- **Collectors / judges:** Gather memories, names, debts, souls, stories, or potential and impose alien rules that weaken local institutions.
- **Independent cosmic predators:** Hunt, feed, migrate, or defend territory without serving Keth-Amar. Keth may exploit their presence, but they can remain dangerous after he retreats.

### 7.4 How a Wyrd Creature Differs from a Similar Native Creature

When a native and Wyrd creature share a body plan, compare five questions before assigning status:

1. **Cosmic origin:** Did it predate the Great Binding as a Mythrill kind, or did an older extra-Mythrill organism arrive through a crack, wake, breach, infected matter, or anchor?
2. **Wyrd dependency:** Can the creature live, reproduce, heal, or remain coherent without Wyrd medium? Native creatures may be supernatural without this dependency.
3. **Anchor:** What specific site, object, host, or route allows it to persist on Mythrill? A native territory is not automatically a Wyrd anchor.
4. **Invasive purpose:** Does it drain, infest, scout, open, weaken, counterfeit, harvest, or siege something in service of a cosmic ecology or Keth-Amar's hunt? A native predator's ecological function is not automatically invasive.
5. **Response to Keth's pressure:** Does it obey, follow, feed alongside, evade, compete with, resist, or ignore Keth-Amar? Independence after his retreat is evidence against treating every Wyrd creature as a servant, not evidence that it is native.

### 7.6 Canon Paragraph for `CORE_LORE_FRAMEWORK.md`

> **Layered Wyrd Ecology:** The native creatures of Mythrill predate the Great Binding and arise from Mythrill's own Old World ecologies, spirits, peoples, and sacred works. The Wyrd is a distinct cosmic medium and corruption associated with Keth-Amar's hunt. Ancient Cosmic Wyrdkin are pre-existing organisms and entities from the dark between stars that may arrive through Aex-shell cracks, Keth-Amar's wake or Astril trail, Wyrd breaches, infected matter, or Aex Shard anchors; they are not all Keth-Amar's offspring. A native creature touched by the Wyrd is an individual or branch condition, not a new species. After Keth-Amar is pushed back, Wyrd creatures may remain stranded in nests, anchors, and breach zones with their own instincts, purposes, factions, and degrees of intelligence.

The exact cosmic habitat name, ancestry, propagation rules, and relationship of each Wyrd kind to Keth-Amar remain provisional until the individual inventory decisions below are ratified.

## 10. Notable Data Inconsistencies

These are the specific issues that should be visible to the next author or implementation pass.

1. **Count/category contradiction:** `rules.json` describes the Bestiary as 21 Wyrd-born creatures, while `creatures.json` contains 193 entries. This plan treats 193 as the complete inventory and separates a Wyrd ecology subset from the narrower direct Keth-spawn subset.
2. **Universal Wyrd framing:** `rules.json`, `CORE_LORE_FRAMEWORK.md`, and several `lore.json` entries imply that folklore beings are created by Wyrd or are universal dark-bargain tolls. That conflicts with native-pre-Binding canon and also erases the distinction between ancient cosmic Wyrdkin, Keth-spawn, and Wyrd-touched natives.
3. **Mixed legacy tags:** 35 JSON records contain a `wyrd-creature` or `Wyrd-kin` tag, while 81 records mention Wyrd in one of the descriptive lore fields. The tag/text boundary does not distinguish native exposure, ancient cosmic Wyrdkin, direct Keth-spawn, or local camouflage.
4. **Exact copied record:** `sirin_song` has the same `origin`, `nature`, `habitat`, and `depth` values as `dziwozona_wild`. It cannot be treated as final Sirin lore.
5. **Mislabeled/copied record:** `kappa` has a Kappa display name and description, but its `origin` begins with "The Sump-Scrab" and its tags include `sump-scrab`. It must be rewritten independently from `sump_scrabs`.
6. **Display-name typo:** JSON ID `oillipheist` is displayed as `Oilliph,ist`; the prose and `lore.json` use `Oillipheist`. The exact current name is preserved in this inventory, but a future correction must choose one canonical display spelling.
7. **Description duplication blemish:** `skerry.description` says "stout, greedy, stout". This is not a lore contradiction by itself, but it indicates rushed or copied prose.
8. **Timeline scale conflict:** `CORE_LORE_FRAMEWORK.md` and `rules.json` use Year 0/300/325/475 and 150 years of Freezing Era, while `Nordhalla_Complete_Lore.md` repeatedly uses eight centuries. This plan uses the framework dates as a working index and records the scale as unresolved.
9. **Nordhalla name mismatch:** `Nordhalla_Complete_Lore.md` lists Jutul-King, Skrei, Huld, Jawl, and Kjarn in regional creature lists, while the JSON inventory contains `jutul`, `skreika`, `huldra`, and no exact Jawl/Kjarn records. The Jutul entry calls out the title question directly.
10. **Old World magic vocabulary:** Several pre-Binding records describe mana, runes, Wyrd, or mortal-style magical effects as though they existed before the Great Binding. These terms need to be reclassified as native resonance, mineral behavior, spirit law, or later house/Wyrd exposure.
11. **Post-Binding candidates:** `bes` is described as crafted by Solari artisans and `hut_ling` as a witch/Wyrd-made chest. They may belong in a deliberate house-era construct category, but they are not automatically native or Wyrdspawn.
12. **Spelling drift:** The sources alternate between `Keth Amar` and `Keth-Amar`, and the house/region material has additional naming drift. Later edits should normalize terms without changing IDs accidentally.
13. **Unresolved cosmic provenance:** The current data has no field for extra-Mythrill origin, cosmic habitat, Wyrd role, anchor/nest, Keth relationship, current independence, or countermeasure. The 20 Wyrd-layer entries therefore use working prose and provisional statuses rather than final taxonomy.
14. **Local-form ambiguity:** Alraune, Sluagh, Stel, Spume of the Drowned, Hungry Child, and Cycle-Eater may be ancient cosmic organisms using local substrates or direct Keth-generated manifestations. The plan preserves both possibilities until anchor, propagation, and dependency evidence is authored.

## 11. Implementation Plan

Do not implement these changes by bulk replacing every mention of Wyrd. The authoring decisions above need to be migrated in stages.

### Phase 1: Lock the canon layer

1. Revise `docs/CORE_LORE_FRAMEWORK.md` to add the Old World native ecology, the no-mortal-magic distinction, the pre-Keth First Cracks/Warmth era, and the layered Wyrd ecology.
2. Replace the universal "living tolls" and folklore-created creature model with native inhabitants, ancient cosmic Wyrdkin, direct Keth-spawn/Wyrdspawn, and Wyrd-touched native conditions. Keep regional bargain effects as environmental pressures, not creature origin rules.
3. Resolve the 475-year versus eight-century calendar before changing dates in creature prose. If the 475-year scale remains, update Nordhalla and dependent lore consistently; if eight centuries remains, update the framework and present-day labels consistently.
4. Preserve optional Old God/native deity relationships as per-entry decisions. Do not add a pantheon name merely to fill an origin field.

### Phase 2: Correct the data foundation

1. Correct `creatures.json` records in this order: `sirin_song`, `kappa`, and the `oillipheist` display spelling. Preserve all IDs and verify that the display-name decision is deliberate.
2. Add or agree on a small metadata contract for the later data revision, such as a lore status, origin class, native baseline flag, Wyrd relationship, and provisional region. Do not add fields until the runtime reader can tolerate them.
3. Rewrite the 20 Wyrd-layer records so each states cosmic provenance, local Wyrd function, Mythrill anchor/nest, Keth-Amar relationship, current independence, and countermeasure. Keep `COSMIC-WYRD-REVIEW` where ancient fauna versus direct spawn is unresolved.
4. Rewrite native and hybrid records without deleting useful folklore, Seelie Accord history, Gref's memory trade, Oilliph(e)ist's river-guardian ancestry, or regional survival practices.
5. Replace pre-Binding mortal-magic wording with native resonance or later exposure where appropriate. Keep actual post-Binding magic effects dated after the house oath.
6. Maintain file order and exact IDs while editing. Use the repository's raw-text JSON workflow: backup, edit, parse, count, compare IDs/names, and leak-check before moving the result into place.

### Phase 3: Reconcile `lore.json`

1. Split native creature entries from explicit Wyrdspawn entries in summaries and `fullEntry` prose.
2. Rework `gref` so the Seelie Accord and memory-merchant identity are native history, with any face-stealing Wyrd form explicitly marked as a later variant or separate entry.
3. Rework `oillipheist` so the river-guardian ancestry is primary and Wyrd exposure is an optional later branch.
4. Keep direct local manifestations such as `the_cinder`, `ash_woven_oracle`, `husque`, and the leading `debt_revenant` classification as Wyrdspawn candidates; treat `gambrel`, `stel`, and other ancient-fauna candidates as cosmic Wyrd statuses unless later evidence proves direct Keth generation.
5. Repair related-term links and regional references after IDs/display names are stable. Preserve the existing canonical region keys.

### Phase 4: Revise `rules.json`

1. Change the Bestiary title/description from an all-Wyrd bestiary to a complete Mythrill bestiary containing native creatures and a marked Wyrd ecology subset.
2. Replace the 21-creature claim with either a generated count or clearly labeled counts for Ancient Cosmic Wyrdkin, direct Wyrdspawn, and Wyrd-touched native variants.
3. Add the player-facing distinction: native creatures predate the Binding; ancient cosmic Wyrdkin predate their arrival on Mythrill; direct Wyrdspawn are generated after Keth-Amar's Wyrd reaches Mythrill; individual native corruption is possible but not universal.
4. Remove the rule that fear creates every creature. Retain the narrower rule that local fear and folklore translate, camouflage, or tactically blueprint Wyrd creatures after arrival.
5. Update regional summaries and adventure hooks so a native creature is not automatically a Wyrd encounter and every cosmic Wyrd encounter has a deliberate anchor, role, degree of independence, and countermeasure.

### Phase 5: Revise class and race lore

1. Audit class lore for assumptions that all monsters, spirits, or supernatural creatures come from Wyrd. Animist, Augur, Warden, Inquisitor, Void-touch, and related classes need distinct relationships to native beings, house magic, and Wyrdspawn.
2. Make Primal, Storm, Rime, Ember, Sacred, Arcane, and Blight relationships describe the Great Binding and later mortal use, not the origin of every creature in the matching region.
3. Give classes that interact with creatures a choice of approach: native treaty, ecological knowledge, house oath, exorcism/severance, or combat. Do not make Wyrd resistance the universal solution.
4. Update race lore where it claims that a house, deity, or Wyrd created an entire creature-kind. Preserve the distinction between playable house lineages and older native peoples.
5. Add class hooks only after the per-creature entries have a stable status, so a class does not canonize a provisional patron or pantheon name.

- Assert that every record has one status, all native authoring fields, and a next decision; assert all cosmic provenance, role, anchor, Keth relationship, independence, and countermeasure fields for Wyrd-layer entries.

- Check `lore.json` related-term links, `rules.json` counts, regional keys, and class/race cross-references.
- Keep this document as the audit/source draft until the revised framework and data are ratified; then update its status and retain it as the historical rationale for the bestiary split.

**Implementation boundary:** This plan intentionally adds no changes to `CORE_LORE_FRAMEWORK.md`, `rules.json`, `creatures.json`, `lore.json`, or class lore. Those files remain untouched until the later revision pass is approved and staged.
