## Astril (astril)

### cardFlavor

Refugees of a devoured star, they carry the last embers of a dead world in their blood, and the guilt of leading the predator to this one.

### overview

The Astril are not of Mythrill. They came from a world that orbited a star called Lumia, a world of farmers and hunters and singers of the soil, until Keth Amar found them. The predator ate Lumia the way it eats every star it finds: slowly, completely, without mercy. The world froze. The sky went dark. Everything that depended on warmth should have died.

The Astril did not die. For ten thousand years before the end, their bloodlines had carried fragments of Lumia's biosphere, living echoes of the star woven into flesh and passed from generation to generation. No one understood why. When Lumia was devoured, the reason became clear: the fragments kept them alive. They held back the cold. They preserved a spark of the dead star's warmth in blood that should have frozen solid.

So the survivors fled. They crossed the void between worlds in vessels built from the wreckage of their own, carrying what remained of Lumia's living fire in their veins. They did not know if they would find anything on the other side of the dark. They went anyway, because the alternative was to lie down on a frozen world and wait for the end.

They found Mythrill. And in finding it, they brought the predator with them. Keth Amar tracks the light it cannot consume, and the Astril glow, faintly, with the light of a star it killed. Their flight across the void was a trail. Their arrival was a beacon. They led the hunger that devoured their sun straight to Sol.

The refugees brought warnings from Lumia. Some interpreters read Selunis's dreams and signs as mandates for protection and a waking rite. Selunis stays dormant; the interpretation does not make her a spoken quest-giver. Aethil's ward prevents Keth's full body from freely entering, while narrower channels carry hostile influence toward the core.

Some communities hide Lumia resonance and ritual sites from a dangerous, fallible predator. Secrecy limits real trails and channels, not an all-knowing creditor. Refugee histories can motivate vigilance, craft, friendship and dissent without making every child guilty for leading Keth here.

But Keth Amar found them anyway.

When it did, the Astril did what they had always done: they carried the warning. They went to the world's leaders again and told them the predator had found its trail. The Binding, they said, would not hold forever. The work must continue. The ritual must continue. And the guilt that the Astril carry, the knowledge that they led the hunger to this world, is the engine that drives every night of their long labor. They are refugees. They are penitents. They are the people who doomed the world they fled to, and they will spend every century they have left trying to save it.

### culturalBackground

Astril communities are small, isolated, and deliberately unremarkable. They farm. They hunt. They keep to themselves. A traveler passing through an Astril village sees hardworking people with strange eyes and quiet manners, nothing more. That is what the Astril want you to see.

Moon-courtyards sustain particular ritual communities. Under Sundrift's starless sky, chart-readers use carried records, internal Lumia patterns and lunar timing; a clear night does not restore visible local constellations. Farming, hunting, scholarship and ordinary family life remain real choices beside the rite.

The night is the teacher. Astril children are born into the work. Before they can walk they learn the feel of moonlight on their skin, the rhythm of the ritual-chants, the names of the stars that mark the hours of the rite. As they grow they learn star-reading, then farming, then hunting, then the deeper mysteries of the Selunis-ritual. They are taught, early and plainly, that they are refugees. That their ancestors came from a world that died. That they led something terrible to this one. That their work, every night, every generation, is the only thing standing between Mythrill and the fate of Lumia. Children raised on this truth do not grow up light-hearted. They grow up purposeful. They grow up carrying a weight the rest of the world does not know is there.

Isolation varies by institution and region. Some settlements guard their rites; others trade, travel, research or seek allies. Keth's knowledge has limits. Leaving a village does not automatically make an Astril an exile or penitent, and inherited resonance is not a universal moral sentence.

They carry guilt the rest of the world does not know about. The leaders of Mythrill know the Astril warned them of Keth Amar. They do not always know, or fully understand, that the Astril are the reason the predator found Sol at all. The Astril themselves never forget. Every ritual performed is penance. Every harvest gathered on foreign soil is a debt. They are farmers and hunters who work the land of a world they doomed, and the land feeds them anyway, and the kindness of that fact is a wound that never quite closes.

### diasporaVariation

Synod Hold remains the principal Lumian institution; Crater-Hold is a martial/craft settlement, not the capital of all Astril. Diaspora farmers, researchers and ritualists preserve different practices. Moon-courtyards and carried charts support travel under a starless sky, without a universal ritual absence timer or a newly visible local constellation.

### meaningfulTradeoffs

Astril gain alien resilience and lunar attunement through Lumia's echo, but their inverted vision blinds them in bright daylight, and their luminous eyes betray every attempt to hide in darkness. The Lumian are so rooted in the physical world that they sometimes forget the ritual that gives their people purpose. The Kordak are so focused on the ritual that they sometimes forget the ground beneath their feet.

### currentCrisis

The Selunis-ritual is approaching a critical phase. The Kordak star-readers have watched the moon's tides shift over the last decade in ways the old charts predicted centuries ago, the signs that the waking is near, perhaps within a generation. But the same decade has seen Keth Amar's awareness grow. The predator stirs more often, reaches further, and the Astril fear it has begun to sense the lunar connection. If the ritual is discovered before the awakening is complete, centuries of nightly work end in a single night. If it is interrupted at the wrong phase, no one knows what happens to a half-woken god. The ritual-tenders are working faster than the charts permit, pushing the rite ahead of its proper rhythm, and the Lumian farmers are digging in, fortifying the remote communities, preparing for the day the predator turns its full attention toward the moon. The Astril have spent centuries hiding. They are beginning to suspect they will not be hidden much longer.

## Fex (fexrick)

### cardFlavor

Master alchemists and cyber-grafters who have been augmenting themselves to endure the Peaks since before the mountain had a name.

### overview

The Fex are not the Groven's keepers; they are their creators, and that is a very different thing. The histories the Groven tell about their makers are true: the vats were real, the serums were real, and the rebellion was real. The Fex lost, and they deserved to lose. What the Groven do not tell, what they cannot know, because they fled upward and never looked back, is that the Fex were already dying when they made them.

The oldest machines in the deepest holdfasts are failing. The maintenance songs that have kept them running for eight millennia have gaps in them now, verses forgotten, refrains degraded into nonsense syllables, whole procedures preserved only in the muscle memory of elders who are dying without apprentices. The Guild-Bound hoard their knowledge like corporate secrets, because proprietary code is the only currency that matters in the neon holdfasts and sharing it means losing a competitive advantage. The Clan-Free survive on what the Guilds discard, salvage, ingenuity, spit, and dangerous homebrew cyberware. Neither faction can fix what is broken. Neither will admit it.

They tinker because they must. They practice alchemy because the Cragjaw's mineral veins offer high-energy compounds found nowhere else, and they have spent millennia learning what they can do. They set traps in the outer tunnels because things live in the deep that predate even them, and they are hungry, and they have been getting bolder. They trade with the surface only through corporate intermediaries, and only when they must, because the surface has never given them anything they could not build themselves, except for raw minerals they cannot reach, and news they do not want.

Their beards are their resumes. Every copper wire, every gear-tooth, every glowing fiber-optic strand announces their craft, their guild, their rank, and their installed cyberware. A Fex's beard is the first thing another Fex reads. It tells them everything they need to know about who they are dealing with, and whether they can trust their code.

Fex preserve some of Mythrill's oldest maintained industrial infrastructure. Deep Alchemists seek radical experiments; the Orthodoxy favors strict preservation; independent workshops repair and improvise. These are institutions and choices, not two universal moral categories. Vurath's stirring and failing machinery are regional crises, while Groven and Sumpborn demand recognition on their own terms.

### culturalBackground

Fex society is built on three ancient pillars, each reinforced by the pressure of millennia underground and transformed by their obsession with self-augmentation.

The first is mechanical tinkering and cybernetics. Not grand engineering, practical, durable, repairable machinery. Gears, pulleys, levers, cable-draws, counterweight elevators, ventilation fans spun by geothermal updraft, and modular clockwork cyberware. Every holdfast hums with the sound of small mechanisms and whirring hydraulics, each one maintained by a Fex who has been tending it for decades. Fex tools are simple enough that any pair of hands can fix them, and elegant enough that a master's work is immediately distinguishable from an apprentice's. Nothing is wasted. Nothing is discarded until it cannot be repaired, and even then, its parts are salvaged for cybernetic components.

The second is neural-alchemy. The Cragjaw Peaks are rich in minerals, crystals, and geothermal compounds found nowhere else on Mythrill. Fex alchemists have spent millennia extracting, refining, and combining these substances into practical solutions: adhesives that bond stone to stone stronger than mortar, serums that neutralize the venom of deep-cave predators, bioluminescent pastes that mark safe tunnel routes, and glowing alchemical cocktails that power their cyber-grafts and interface directly with their nervous systems.

The third is cunning, the goblin-gnome inheritance that keeps them alive in a hostile world. The outer tunnels of every holdfast are labyrinths of traps: pressure-plates that trigger deadfalls, tripwires that release sleep-gas from alchemical reserves, echo-chambers that make a dozen Fex sound like a hundred. Invaders get lost. Predators get killed. The Fex do not fight fair because fair fights are for people who can afford to lose. They trade with the surface through intermediaries, never directly, and they never reveal where their holdfast entrances are. They record their history in knot-strings and gear-glyphs, not writing, which can be stolen and read, but physical records that only a trained Fex can interpret. They compete through craft: annual gear-fairs where holdfasts display their finest cyberware inventions.

The Guilds operate like neon-lit industrial syndicates. Membership is hereditary. Techniques are passed from elder to apprentice in strict oral tradition, never written, never shared outside the guild. A Guild-Bound Fex's beard tells their entire professional history at a glance: copper wire for tinkering guilds, silver filament for alchemy guilds, braided leather for trap-craft guilds, gear-teeth woven in for each rank achieved, and fiber-optic indicators for high-level neural integrations. The Guilds hoard knowledge jealously, and a holdfast whose turbine fails will not receive help from a neighboring holdfast whose guild possesses the relevant maintenance song.

The Clan-Free are Fex outside the guild system. Some are descendants of exiles. Some are from holdfasts that collapsed, their guilds destroyed, their knowledge lost. Some chose freedom over security, self-taught tinkerers, independent alchemists, Fex who survive on ingenuity, salvage, and dangerous homebrew cyber-grafts. Their beards are simpler, improvised, unique. The Guild-Bound view them as untrained and unreliable. The Clan-Free view the Guild-Bound as gatekeepers sitting on knowledge they do not deserve. Both are right.

Their architecture is practical, exposed, and glowing. Nothing is hidden behind walls in a Fex holdfast, because you might need to fix it. Pipes run openly along ceilings. Gears and valves are visible, accessible, and cleaned regularly. Elevator-cranks occupy the center of every major chamber. Ventilation shafts are wide enough to crawl through for maintenance, and narrow enough that nothing larger than a Fex can follow. The holdfasts descend around geothermal vents in tiered galleries, lit by the orange glow of volcanic heat and the cold blue pulse of bioluminescent mineral deposits and alchemical indicator lights. The sound of hammering echoes through every level. The air moves, always. A Fex holdfast with still air is a holdfast in crisis.

### diasporaVariation

The Fex are the most widely diasporic non-human race because they built, and maintain, the geothermal infrastructure connecting every region. Brasskin guild-halls exist in every capital (Merrowport, Greymark, Atropolis, the Frozen Archive, Harath-Vault, Synod Hold), each staffed by Brasskin who have lived local for generations and speak the local tongue with a Fex accent. These diaspora Brasskin are culturally flexible in a way the Frostmaw-pure Brasskin distrust. The Alchemite clan-nomads roam between all regions on maintenance-circuits and are functionally a trans-national people, an Alchemite speaks seven regional dialects and claims no capital.

### meaningfulTradeoffs

Fex craft and oral knowledge pair with conductive-graft vulnerability and the recorded Still-Air limitations. Guild secrecy, Groven relations and Sumpborn recognition offer political conflicts without making every engineer responsible for every experiment.

### currentCrisis

The oldest machines are failing faster than the oral traditions can be recovered. In the last century alone, three primary turbines, each over five thousand years old, have begun making sounds not documented in any maintenance song. The Guilds debate endlessly: send expeditions to recover lost knowledge from sealed holdfasts, or authorize the Deep Alchemists to attempt experimental repairs. The Clan-Free have begun improvising solutions on their own, using salvage and untested alchemy. The Orthodoxy condemns this as dangerous. The Deep Alchemists watch from their sump-laboratories, waiting for the moment when desperation makes their methods acceptable again. In the deepest level of the oldest holdfast, the First Turbine has begun making a sound like someone trying to remember a song they have not heard in eight thousand years. But last month, for the first time, a secondary turbine in the Fourth Holdfast answered it. The machines are not just failing. They are beginning to communicate. And the frequency they are using is not in any maintenance song; it is a language the Fex have never needed to understand because nothing alive has spoken it since before the holdfasts were carved.

**description:** The Brasskin are retrofuturistic artisan engineers born into the corporate guild system, inheritors of formal training, ancient proprietary oral traditions, and the proud aesthetic of exposed, precision-machined brass. Heavily influenced by an industrial diving-suit and art-deco workshop aesthetic, they wear heavy brass diving-suit goggles with swiveling magnifying loupes, analog needle pressure gauges, and chest-mounted pressure regulators. Their beards are meticulously braided with copper wire, gear-teeth, and fiber-optic strands. Every Brasskin sports visible mechanical prosthetics—articulated brass arms with pneumatic piston joints, exposed escapement ratchets, or whirring clockwork tickers in their chest cavities—awarded by guild syndicates as both trade credential and status symbol. Their hands are precise. Their minds are methodical. The Guild-Bound Brasskin trace their lineage to Brasskin Gear-Father, who founded the first holdfast eight millennia before the Freezing Era, maintaining oral maintenance songs that are the oldest continuous knowledge transmission in the known world.

**culturalBackground:** Brasskin culture is built on intellectual property preservation. They maintain the old songs exactly as they were taught, follow security protocols without deviation, and believe the Fex's survival depends on guarding what remains of the original knowledge. They are not wrong. The oral traditions are the only record of how the oldest machines function, and every deviation introduces error, and errors compound over millennia. A Brasskin who innovates is a Brasskin who has admitted the old ways are insufficient, and that admission is the first crack in the foundation of guild authority.

Guild hierarchy is rigid and hereditary. Children are apprenticed to their parents' guild syndicate at age five. Promotion is slow, based on demonstrated mastery and political patience. A Brasskin elder has spent two centuries accumulating the copper wires and gear-teeth in their beard, and they do not share hard-won blueprints with juniors who have not yet earned it. The Craft-Hoarding is not cruelty. It is corporate tradition. A technique shared too freely loses value. A formula given without proper apprenticeship is a formula wasted.

Kethrin dominate the holdfast councils, control access to the best machine-tending positions, and speak for the Fex in what little trade occurs with the surface. They view the Clan-Free with a mixture of pity and contempt, untrained, undisciplined, unpredictable. They do not see that the Clan-Free are fixing machines the Guilds have already written off as lost.

**currentCrisis:** The First Turbine is dying. The oldest machine in the oldest holdfast, the one that has been running since before speech was invented, has begun emitting a grinding harmonic that vibrates through the entire geothermal grid. The Brasskin guild councils have quarantined the turbine chamber and forbidden discussion, but the harmonic is spreading through the pipes. Every holdfast feels it now. The Guild-Bound are hoarding their maintenance songs tighter than ever, convinced that revealing the full scope of the failure would trigger a panic that could collapse Fex civilization. The older Brasskin know the songs are incomplete. The younger Brasskin know the elders are hiding something. Neither generation trusts the other enough to share what they know, and the turbine's song is getting louder.

They live in the deep sump-tunnels, salvaging the chemical runoffs discarded by the corporate holdfasts above and transforming toxic waste into subterranean life.

**culturalBackground:** Alchemite culture is built on survival chemistry, subterranean botany, and alchemical rebellion. A Alchemite workshop is a chaotic laboratory filled with glass flasks, coils of rubber siphon tubing, acid-stained slate tables, and hand-etched copper syringe kits. The Alchemite must decide whether to absorb the guild refugees, turn them away, or ally with the one faction every Fex has sworn never to trust again.
