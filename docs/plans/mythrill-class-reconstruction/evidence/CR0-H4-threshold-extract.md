## Athien (neth)

### cardFlavor

The pale-skinned Pact-Lords, bound by an ancient contract to Morvane, writing new agreements to sustain their pale skin and stilled breath.

### overview

The Athien were dying. The first freeze had crept into the Gloom, not the sudden ice of Nordhalla, but a slow, insidious cold that seeped through the ancient woods and found the children first. Their ancestors were scribes, archivists, merchant-brokers, the Gloom's legal class, the ones who recorded who owed whom, who married whom, who betrayed whom, who inherited what. They were not warriors or priests. They were the people who remembered, and memory does not stop frostbite.

They walked into the deep wood where the bioluminescent trees thin and the preserving bog begins, carrying every contract they had ever written, every precedent, and every genealogical record. They found the Morvane waiting at the boundary, an entity older than the Wyrd, older than the noble families, and older than any name. The Mycellan call it the Root-Veil and worship it. The Athien ancestors called it by what it is: the thing that decides what the Gloom preserves and what the Gloom consumes.

They did not pray. They presented a case.

*You are the record-keeper of the forest. The Athien are the record-keepers of civilization. If we die, the record dies, every debt, every promise, every name. If you preserve us, we will record everything. Every contract we write will carry a piece of your authority. The forest's memory will never fade, because we will be its living archive.*

Morvane had never been reasoned with, only feared, prayed to, or fed. It considered the argument, the first argument anyone had ever made to it that treated it as a partner, not a spirit, and accepted.

They rose from the bog with pale skin instead of dying flesh, with stilled breath, and with the pact written in the blood of every descendant, a contract that would outlast their bodies and carry into subsequent generations. Morvane would preserve them for as long as they honored the terms.

That was before the sun was buried. The First Contract still rests in the heart of Atropolis, visible through living heartwood like a fossil in amber. The Athien are still there, and they are still writing. Morvane is still watching, not as a spirit or predator, but as an ancient, unyielding sovereign of precedent with eternal patience and a perfect memory for breached terms. The Athien do not die; they renegotiate.

The **Well of Life** liturgy tells the same founding event as Vaelis's First Contract: limited faithful use and raw over-drawing explain Athien and Weft. Riven arise later through deliberate Severing, not a third original Well response. Scripture and statute preserve different views of one negotiation with native Morvane, whose reach depends on actual channels and covenants.

### culturalBackground

Athien society is built on a single document: the First Contract, preserved in the heart of Atropolis. Every Athien contract written since carries a fragment of that original pact's authority, a tiny renewal of the agreement that keeps their entire species alive. They do not write contracts because they love paperwork. They write contracts because the act of writing one is an act of worship, a reminded pulse sent through the pact, a signal to Morvane that says: *we are still here, and the terms still hold.*

This has produced a civilization whose every institution is mediated by negotiated terms. Athien parents write birth-contracts with their newborns, care obligations, filial duties, inheritance clauses. Athien lovers negotiate relationship-documents with renewal options and dissolution penalties. The most intimate bond in Athien society is not a marriage. It is a forty-seven-page cohabitation agreement that both parties update annually.

To outsiders, this reads as cold, transactional, alienating. To the Athien, it is the opposite: a contract is the most sincere form of love because it says *I am willing to be bound to you in a way Morvane will enforce.* They do not understand why other races find this monstrous.

The deeper flaw is not the contract-spiral itself. It is what the contract-spiral produces over centuries: **amber.** The pact preserved the Athien as they were at the moment of signing, and preservation resists change. The eldest Athien are magnificent, unchanging, frozen in their first century's patterns. They repeat phrases their original mouths spoke four hundred years ago. They maintain grudges their original counterparts have been dead for centuries. They are not senile. They are *perfectly preserved.* And completely, utterly still.

Young Athien, those only fifty or sixty years old, still fluid, still capable of surprise, rage against the weight of centuries. They want to invent, to change, to become something the pact didn't account for. This creates a silent generational war that has been running for five hundred years without either side acknowledging it aloud.

**The Unraveling** is what happens when a Athien decides to break contracts on purpose, not for gain, but to force change. It begins small: a vendor's receipt ignored, a casual promise broken, a ritual obligation skipped. At first it feels like freedom. The pact loosens. The Athien becomes more fluid, spontaneous, briefly *alive.* Then the Fading begins, because breaking contracts is breaking the pact that sustains them. Most Athien who start the Unraveling stop before it's fatal, returning to the contract-spiral chastened and hollow. Some don't stop. Some would rather die fluid than live frozen.

The Athien operate from their grave-city Atropolis, a cathedral-grove woven into the canopy of the Gloom's oldest ironwood forest. They control the major trade outposts, Ironjaw Port on the Iceheart Sea, the Frostwood trade-embassy, the Sundale crossroads, because these outposts house copies of the First Contract. Without a copy nearby, the Athien begin the Fading: a slow unraveling of mind and body that ends in a pale-skinned husk with nothing left behind the eyes. Their trade empire is not built on ambition. It is built on necessity. They cannot leave the documents, so the documents must go wherever they go.

### diasporaVariation

The Athien diaspora is the most institutionally structured of any race: every outpost (Ironjaw Port, the Frostwood trade-embassy, the Sundale crossroads) houses a copy of the First Contract and a Athien garrison to tend it. Each outpost has developed a distinct character: Ironjaw-Port Athien are maritime-minded and feuding over tariff policy; the Frostwood-embassy Athien have grown fog-tolerant and keep dual journals (Sovereign Ledger and First Contract); the Sundale-crossroads Athien run the hottest (geothermal proximity slows the Fading) and are the most likely to Unravel. The Athien-dominated outposts are rigid; the Weft frontier-posts are fluid and experimental.

### meaningfulTradeoffs

Athien gain preservation and magical power through their pact with Morvane, but are tethered to physical copies of the First Contract, straying too far causes the Fading. The Unraveling offers freedom at the cost of self-destruction. Subrace flaws are binding: Athien are bound by Morvane's authority and face harsh consequences for breach, Weft break things they cannot see, and Riven are legally non-existent.

### currentCrisis


Three things are troubling the Athien, and none of them have been spoken aloud in the contract-halls of Atropolis.

The first is Morvane's attention. The elders who have returned many times, the ones whose death-count exceeds ten, who have stood at the threshold often enough to know Morvane's presence by its weight, report that something has changed. Morvane is distant. Colder. Absent for longer periods, as if something else has its attention, something larger, something elsewhere, something that is making the preservation of one forest-boundary entity and its contract-bound partners seem like a smaller priority than it used to be. The younger Athien cannot feel this. They have nothing to compare it to. But the elders are afraid, and the elders do not frighten easily.

The second is the Unraveling. The number of Athien who have begun the Unraveling, breaking contracts in sequence, feeling the temporary freedom, inching toward dissolution, has tripled in the last fifty years. No one has published the statistics. The statistics exist; the Weft can read them in the obligation-web, and the Athien can find them in the Canopy-Ledger if they look. The cause is unclear. Some blame Morvane's distraction, a weaker pact is easier to break. Some blame the cultural stagnation, the young are rebelling because the old have been frozen for centuries and show no sign of thawing. Some blame the Severing itself, every name burned from the First Contract weakens the pact slightly, and there have been more Severings in the last century than in the three centuries before.

The third is the bloodline-fracture. The three subraces have always existed in tension, but that tension has been functional for centuries beyond counting. Now it is becoming something else. The Athien are consolidating power, they control the contract-halls, the trade delegations, the external diplomacy. The Weft are withdrawing, fewer of them attend the contract-halls, fewer of them submit their probability-predictions for official record. The Riven are multiplying, the Severing is becoming less rare, less stigmatized, more visible, and the Athien have no legal mechanism to stop it because the Severing is not illegal. It simply removes the severer from legal existence, which makes prosecution impossible.

Somewhere in the deep bog, a Riven elder, perhaps Saren-Vel herself, perhaps someone who has never spoken their name aloud, is preparing an argument. No one knows what it argues. No one knows to whom it is addressed. But the Weft can feel it in the obligation-web: a new clause forming, a new contract being drafted, a new case being prepared. And the defendant has not yet been named.
 

### Nethien — velun_neth

Native: Animist, Arcanoneer, Revenant, Chronarch, Spellguard.

**description:** The original pact-bound Athien bloodline, predating later Riven severance. They cannot knowingly make false pact-enforced assertions; withholding, conditional statements and literal loopholes are distinct. Morvane enforces actual First Contract obligations, not every unrelated patron's bargain or every hypothetical statement. Their preserved bodies and spoken clauses carry the partnership's authority within its scope.

**culturalBackground:** The Athien trace their bloodline to the original scribes who presented the First Contract to Morvane, Vaelis the Scribe's direct descendants, generation by generation, clause by clause. They run Atropolis. They write the contracts that govern Athien society. They negotiate with the Solari, the humans, the Mimir, anyone who needs Athien preservation-craft or Athien memory-glass, and their negotiations are always, always binding.

Athien children are educated in the contract-halls from the moment their pale skin stabilizes, usually around age five or six. They learn to read ancient scrolls and contract covenants before they learn to read spoken faces. They learn contract law before they learn ethics. They learn that every word they speak carries weight, and that weight accumulates, a careless promise at ten becomes a binding obligation at fifty, and Morvane does not distinguish between a child's protestation and an adult's oath.

A Athien's power is their voice. When they invoke a clause aloud, a binding, a sealing, a cosmic enforcement, the pact responds. The air thickens. The light shifts. Everyone in the room feels Morvane's attention settle on the proceedings like a held breath. A Athien who has perfected their craft can seal a contract with a single spoken sentence that no signatory can ever break, not without consequences that ripple across generations.

The flaw is inseparable from the gift: a knowingly false pact-enforced assertion is forbidden, and an actual obligation carries consequences. Withholding, literal loopholes, and conditional statements require careful wording but are not automatically new obligations. First Contract jurisdiction is bounded; it does not turn every child's protest or hypothetical into an omniscient universal contract.

They view the Weft with a mixture of admiration and unease, the Weft see the web behind the text, the implications the Athien cannot perceive, and that makes them useful and unpredictable. They view the Riven with something closer to horror, the Riven are what happens when a Athien decides the pact's weight is heavier than the silence, and the Athien cannot process a choice that results in legal non-existence.

## Mycellan (vreken)

### cardFlavor

Amber-eyed crypt-keepers who tend the Sunken Spire reverse cathedrals beneath the Bryngloom bogs.

### culturalBackground

Mycellan culture is monastic, alternative, and deeply rooted in their bond with the wounded land. Two institutions shape every aspect of their society: the crypt and the spore.

The crypt is their social institution. Mycellan do not bury their dead in earth. They entomb them in inverted cathedrals carved into the Gloom's peat-stone, vaulted chambers lit only by the fungal shrouds of ancestors whose bodies still emit light generations after death. The Sunken Spires descend into the earth rather than rising toward the sky, their spires pointing downward into the dark peat-stone, each spiraling gallery older and deeper than the last. A family's status is measured by how many generations their crypt still glows. Wealth is measured in ancestral light. Every Mycellan cathedral is also a mausoleum, the walls lined with preserved dead whose fungal wrappings continue to emit the lantern-glow of their bloodline. Priests, called Veil-Speakers, sing the names of the crypt's dead in endless rotation, so that no light is ever truly extinguished.

The spore is their sustenance and their craft. The Mycellan cultivate phosphorescent fungi in their crypt-cathedrals for food, medicine, light, and ritual purposes. Spore-gardens line the lower galleries of every Sunken Spire, tended by keepers who can read a fungal bloom's health by the color of its glow. They are stealthy, centuries of navigating the dark, dangerous Bryngloom have made them masters of concealment, and their approach to problems is unconventional: where other races meet obstacles with force or negotiation, the Mycellan circumvent, subvert, or simply wait until the obstacle decomposes.

The Over-Lit are the wound in Mycellan society. The phosphorescent fungi the Mycellan cultivate are not merely a resource, they are intoxicating. Prolonged exposure to concentrated fungal glow induces a euphoric, sedative state called "the hush." In moderation, the ambient light of caverns and crypts is harmless, even meditative. But concentrated doses, raw fungal pulp pressed against the skin, inhaled spore-vapor, direct ingestion, produce a deeper, addictive high. The Over-Lit are Mycellan who chased the hush too far. Their lantern-eyes burn bright at all times, they can no longer dim them. Their bodies grow gaunt, their movements twitchy, their skin pale and translucent as the glow eats through them from within. They cluster in shanties at the edges of settlements, tolerated, pitied, feared. Every Mycellan knows someone who fell. Every Cromyx, those of the Ghost-Mycelium bloodline, whose sensitivity makes them uniquely vulnerable, worries it could be them.

The Inquisitors are the Mycellan who hunt their own. When an Over-Lit progresses beyond recovery, when the hush has hollowed them out completely and they have become a danger to others, the Inquisitors are called. They are Cromyx who have trained themselves to track their own kind through the deep caves by spore-sign and sound. They are feared, respected, and deeply isolated. To kill a kinsman, even one already lost to the hush, is a burden that no Bedel, those of the Deep-Glow bloodline, less vulnerable to the hush, can truly understand.

Mycellan architecture is gothic monastic: low, arched stone halls built around central crypt-courtyards where ancestral light provides the only illumination. Vaulted ceilings. Narrow lancet windows that admit no outside light, the interior glows from within. Settlements are organized like abbeys: chapter house, infirmary, scriptorium, crypt. Every structure descends rather than rises, the spires of a Mycellan cathedral reach into the earth, not toward the sky. Their most sacred spaces are the deepest, closest to the Root-Veil: the mycelial network beneath the Gloom that the Mycellan believe is Morvane's wounded nervous system, the conduit through which their deity still, faintly, lives.

The Athien and the Mycellan share the Bryngloom Forest and barely speak. The Athien arrived centuries after the corruption began, a frail dying people fleeing the surface freeze who made their own desperate bargain with the same Morvane whose suffering had already transformed the Mycellan, and profited from it. The Athien got pale skin and immortality. The Mycellan, who had been suffering for centuries, got nothing. The Athien view the Mycellan as archaic victims, a people who surround themselves with glowing dead relatives when they could be running a trade empire. The Mycellan view the Athien as spiritual parasites, profiting from the same deity whose wound still bleeds through Mycellan veins. Their relationship is coldly functional: trade routes, shared borders, fungal-light exports to Athien grave-cities in exchange for bog-preserved goods and cold-iron tools. Neither defers to the other. Neither particularly wants to.

The Solari of Sundale are the Mycellan's silent kindred. The Sol's Breath, the eternal ember the Solari tend, and the Root-Veil the Mycellan venerate emerged from the same era: the memory of warmth in a world that froze. Solari forge-pilgrims occasionally journey to Mycellan crypt-cathedrals. Mycellan spire-tenders occasionally travel to Sundale to trade cultivation techniques. Neither race speaks openly of what they recognize in each other. The recognition is enough.

To the humans of the surrounding regions, the Mycellan are the grave-lights: lantern-eyed keepers who live in upside-down cathedrals and sing to their dead. Their spore-craft is the finest in the known world. Their medicines work where others fail. But being treated by a Mycellan means lying on a stone slab in a mausoleum, ancestors glowing softly from every wall, while someone with too many joints and silver-glowing eyes tends a poultice of something that is still, faintly, alive. It works. It is unsettling. And the humans come anyway, because the alternative is worse. The Morren, the Bryngloom's old human population, once walked the same bogs; the blight of their bargain absorbed them into the Mycellan, and the debt they carried survives in the Mycellan's contracts to this day.

### diasporaVariation

Mycellan diaspora is sparse but old, lantern-eyed crypt-keepers serve as spore-cultivators, healers, and ancestral-consultants in every region's burial grounds. A Nordhalla-Mycellan (serving the Frozen Archive's glacier-tombs) has adapted to the cold their southern kin cannot bear, their bioluminescence dimmed to a faint blue that the glacier-ice amplifies. A Sundale-Mycellan is rare and grim, the volcanic heat distresses their fungal-bonded skin, and they cluster in the deep ash-tombs beneath the Ashen Escarpment. The Bryngloom remains the only region with a large Mycellan population; elsewhere they are solitary practitioners, respected, avoided, and slowly losing their Root-Veil connection the further they roam from the wounded land that made them.

### meaningfulTradeoffs

Mycellan gain unparalleled perception of the Root-Veil and the underground world through their fungal bond, but their bioluminescent eyes make stealth difficult and their tie to a wounded god carries a physical and emotional toll. The Cromyx live with the constant threat of the Over-Lit addiction.

### currentCrisis

The central crisis is the deepening of Morvane's wound, and the fear that the Root-Veil's slow failure will accelerate the Mycellan's devolution past any hope of recovery. The **Ghost-Mycelium** grows brighter and more volatile each generation, the Cromyx read this as Morvane's pain worsening, the Bedel read it as the corruption spreading. The **Cromyx** have begun the **Bright-Speaking**, a heretical movement demanding scriptorium access and an end to the Bedel's monopoly on leadership, arguing that those who feel Morvane's wound most closely should be the ones tending the cure. Tensions are peaking as a Cromyx coven prepares to force entry into the deepest galleries of the Hollow-Scriptorium to find records of a failed rite that some believe could cleanse the Root-Veil, while Athien merchants watch the instability, preparing to exploit any slip in the fungal-light trade, and quietly aware that if Morvane dies, their own First Contract dies with it.

### Bedel — clean_vreken

Native: Animist, Plaguebringer, Revenant.

**description:** The inheritors of Deep-Glow, the oldest and most stable fungal strain, bonded to their ancestors in the deep caves before the Wyrd wounded Morvane. Their transformation was less severe: their features are closer to their original elven forms, their lantern-eyes burn a controlled rust-amber, warm and steady, easily dimmed in polite company. The Deep-Glow strain threads less deeply through their nervous systems, leaves their minds more stable, their bodies more composed. They are near-total immune to the Over-Lit addiction: not morally superior, merely less entangled in Morvane's wound, carrying a strain that does not intoxicate. Disproportionately represented in trade delegations, crypt-councils, scriptorium gatekeeping, and every position that faces the outside world. Their privilege is invisible to them, they have never had to fight the hush, so they do not understand why the Cromyx cannot simply resist it. The flaw they carry is the quiet assumption that their biological luck is a form of virtue.

**culturalBackground:** The Bedel trace their bloodline to the first Mycellan who carried Deep-Glow, the stable, warm-amber fungal strain that threads least deeply through the nervous system and produces steady light with minimal intoxication. Their tradition is continuity: the scriptoriums, the crypt-councils, the trade routes, the diplomatic missions to Athien grave-cities and Solari forge-halls. The Bedel are the face the Mycellan show to outsiders, and they have been the face for so long that they have begun to forget there are other Mycellan in the dark who look nothing like them.

Bedel children are educated in the scriptoriums from the age when their lantern-eyes first steady, around six or seven. They learn to read fungal-wood records, to recite crypt-genealogies, to tend the spore-gardens and cultivate the strains that sustain the monastery. By adolescence they are expected to choose a vocation: Veil-Speaker (priesthood), Inscriptor (record-keeper), Witch Doctor (spore-healer), or Inquisitor (hunter). The last is the least common among the Bedel, their senses are less acute than the Cromyx's, and hunting an Over-Lit through the deep caves with dimmer eyes is a test few Bedel volunteer for.

They view the Cromyx with a complex, carefully unspoken mixture of pity and fear. The Cromyx are their siblings, their cousins, their children who inherited a different strain. The Bedel recognize the Cromyx's sharper perception of the Root-Veil. They rely on it, the best cultivators and readers of the underground in Mycellan society are overwhelmingly Cromyx. But the Bedel also control the institutions that record, govern, and trade, and none of those institutions have a Cromyx majority. The Bedel call this "stability." The Cromyx call it what it is.

The Bedel rarely leave the Bryngloom Forest. When they do, they travel in monastic vestments, dark cassocks with fungal-thread embroidery marking their bloodline and vocation, and their rust-amber eyes draw stares in human settlements, Athien trade-hubs, and Solari forge-halls alike. The Bedel are accustomed to being stared at. They are not accustomed to being doubted.

### Cromyx — marked_vreken

Native: Inquisitor, Plaguebringer, Apex, Augur.

**description:** The inheritors of Ghost-Mycelium, Morvane's wounded nerve-tissue made symbiotic, the volatile, brilliant, painful strain that amplifies perception and sensitivity at the cost of stability. Their lantern-eyes burn silver-white and do not dim easily; their emotions are readable from across a room, their presence impossible to hide in darkness. Their perception of the Root-Veil is extraordinary: they sense the mycelial network's pulse more clearly than any Bedel, read the spore-signatures of the underground, detect things other Mycellan cannot. They are also the most vulnerable to the Over-Lit addiction, their sensitivity is both gift and curse, and every Cromyx family carries at least one Over-Lit relative. Stigmatized by the Bedel as unreliable, dangerous, the bloodline most deeply entangled in the wounded god. The flaw they carry is their own biology: the light that makes them extraordinary is the same light that consumes them from within.

**culturalBackground:** The Cromyx trace their bloodline to the Mycellan most deeply touched by Morvane's wound, those who carry Ghost-Mycelium, the brilliant, silver-white fungal strain that is the deity's injured nerve-tissue made symbiotic. Their tradition is sensitivity: the Root-Veil read at acute depth, the spore-signatures of the underground sensed where the Bedel perceive only faint hum, the Over-Lit kin tracked into the deep caves by the same bond that will one day betray the tracker.

Cromyx children learn to feel the Root-Veil before they learn to speak, their Ghost-Mycelium bond is active from birth, silver-white pinpoints flickering in the cradle. By the time a Cromyx child can walk, they are already attuned to the mycelial network's pulse: they sense the health of the fungal gardens, the shift in the bog's currents, the distant tremor of something moving through the deep caves. They are taught to dim their eyes, a skill the Bedel never need to practice, and most never fully master it. A Cromyx's lantern-eyes flicker with their emotions, flare with their anger, dim with their grief. They are the most honest people in the Bryngloom Forest, and the most exposed.

Every Cromyx family has at least one Over-Lit relative. The numbers are not a matter of debate, they are recorded in the scriptoriums, which are maintained by the Bedel. The Cromyx believe the Bedel underreport the epidemic to minimize its political impact. The Bedel believe the Cromyx exaggerate their suffering to gain exploit in crypt-council negotiations. Neither side has proof. Both sides have centuries of resentment.

The Cromyx are disproportionately represented among the Inquisitors, the order that hunts Over-Lit Mycellan who have progressed past recovery. This is not because the Bedel forbid Bedel from joining. It is because the Bedel's fainter Root-Veil sense makes them less effective trackers, and because the psychological burden of killing your own kind falls more heavily on a Bedel who has never felt the hush's pull than on a Cromyx who has spent their entire life fighting it. The Cromyx who join the Inquisition are the most respected and most isolated Mycellan in the Gloom, feared by the Over-Lit they hunt, pitied by the Bedel who no longer sit beside them at crypt-feasts, and quietly, desperately envied by the Cromyx who have not yet fallen.

The Cromyx rarely serve as trade delegates or diplomatic envoys. Their eyes are too bright, they unsettle human merchants, make Athien negotiators uncomfortable, and remind the Solari of something their Sun-Speakers have been prophesying for centuries but will not name. When a Cromyx leaves the Bryngloom Forest, it is because they have chosen to, and the choice marks them as someone who has accepted that the outside world will stare and flinch and reach for iron that does not burn them but still, somehow, hurts to look at.
