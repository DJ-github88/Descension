# MYTHRILL CLASS LORE COMPENDIUM

## Complete Lore Reference for All 21 Playable Classes

> **Status:** Canonical Master Lore Reference (era-relative Master Timeline of the Freezing Era).  
> **Master Framework:** Every class discipline draws power from the 8 primal magics (Ember, Blight, Sacred, Arcane, Storm, Rime, Primal, Wyrd), glowing Aex Shard catalysts, ancestral blood pacts, or mechanical engineering.

---

## CLASS HERITAGE TAXONOMY

Every class discipline in Mythrill is rooted in one of five distinct historical and physical origins:

1. **Pre-Binding Classes (Traditional / Mortals):** Disciplines forged when mortals had zero magic. Built on cold-forged steel, black-powder matchlocks, bear traps, archery, and anatomical conditioning (*Berserker*, *Apex*, *Minstrel*, *Warden*, *Toxicologist*).
2. **Celestial Bargain Classes (Infused Houses):** Elemental control granted through the blood pact struck with the celestial guardians Aex and Aethil (*Spellguard*, *Arcanoneer*, *Chronarch*, *Shaper*, *Crusader*). *Martyr:* the devotional channel of Aex's sacrifice, a Witness trained to stay lucid inside pain.
3. **Vreken Extortion (Blight Magic):** The fungal cave-houses extorted divine desperation for raw power, receiving Blight magic that rotted their noble bloodlines into sulfurous bogs (*Blight-Weaving and Decay disciplines*).
4. **Cosmic Collision Classes (Post-Keth Amar):** Forged when the void predator Keth Amar shattered the celestial shell at Blizzard's End:
   * **Harbinger:** Doom-arithmetic converting reality tears into kinetic force.
   * **Plaguebringer:** Void decay grafted directly into living bacterial hosts.
   * **Revenant:** Souls anchored between volcanic embers and frozen stasis.
   * **False Prophet:** Psychic channels tuned to the screaming silence of dead stars.
   * **Lunarch:** Cold-starlight parasites nesting inside the bone marrow.
   * **Inquisitor:** Occult investigators using silver needles and bound Wyrd mimics.
   * **Animist:** Walking archives carrying the physical mutations of three dead traditions.
   * **Augur:** Gutter seers carving fresh animal entrails to read the next ten seconds of combat.
5. **Hazard Pact Classes (The Debt-Bound):** Disciplines whose power is a wager or debt against a cosmic entity, staked at the point of irreversible catastrophe:
   * **Gambit:** The House's line of credit, written in stolen luck and collected as Karmic Debt. Mael-Zhul keeps the ledger.
   * **Pyrofiend:** Claimed by the Emberspire collision and indentured to Scathrach, the will that grew out of the fire. A Cosmic Collision product whose mechanism is a hazard-pact debt.

# ANIMIST

**Origin:** Three centuries ago, three survivalists from different corners of the world developed identical blood-magic by accident. In the southern plains, an Ordan scout sat in the dirt for three seasons until calcified bone spurs tore through his elbows, tuning his body to wind spirits. In the northern fungal bogs, a Vreken herbalist inhaled glowing spore dust to hear her dead mother’s voice. In the frozen archives, a Skald scholar carved clockwork equations into his ribs. When the three crossed paths at a trading post, their bone spurs vibrated to the same acoustic frequency. They realized their separate traditions were dialects of a single language spoken by the dead.

**Identity:** A walking archive of three dead cultures, etched in jagged scar tissue and calcified bone spurs. Animists do not chant incantations; their bodies violently mutate. Sharp bone totems erupt through their forearms. Phosphorescent glyphs ignite across their skin, and ancestral voices scream through continuous waking hallucinations. Every shred of borrowed power accelerates Spirit Erosion—the more ancestral resonance you channel, the faster the spirits devour your sanity.

**Role in World:** Living siege anchors and wilderness guides. Animists drive heavy bone totems into the dirt, lace defensive perimeters with glowing runic nets, and call down curses on enemy lines. Frontier outposts pay gold for their warding rites, but civilian towns bar their gates against them: the bone spurs, skin glyphs, and loud arguments with invisible ghosts terrify common folk.

**Cultural Affiliation:** Practiced by Ordan steppe-riders, Earthen and Brutish Astril refugees, Skald runic scholars, Morgh Groven vat-choirs, Waste-Solari ash-choirs, and the Florae grove-cantors of both bloodlines.

**Sites:** The Concord of Tongues (the Frozen Archive collation hall where the dialects are argued into one lexicon), the Listening Mound (Sundrift Vale, where the oldest dead still answer cleanly), Nyssa's Sink (the Bryngloom spore-hollow of the first deliberate inhalation), and the Quenched Hearth (a burned Sky-Singer steading in Nordhalla, silent to every dialect).

*Eight Cultural Variants:*
* **Ordan ("The Steppe-Throat"):** Sings ancestral voices through double-tone throat singing, weaving ghost-voices into migration horsehair.
* **Vashir Astril ("The Heritage-Conduit"):** Channels the biological memories of a shattered alien world preserved in crystalline blood cells.
* **Silath Astril ("The Star-Communer"):** Stands motionless in the moon-courtyard and lets the dead star speak through their crystalline markings.
* **Skald ("The Rune-Keeper"):** Carves the names of dead warlords into scar tissue, using raised skin as a permanent dwelling for the spirit.
* **Morgh Groven ("The Vat-Mouth"):** Listens to the alchemical dead still arguing in the calcified substrate of drained vats, taking their last testimony onto slate.
* **Waste-Solari ("The Ash-Choir"):** Reads funeral ash-fall on the caldera slopes, where the ancestors answer in ember-crack and soot-pattern.
* **Viridian Florae ("The Thorn-Cantor"):** Gives voice to the Unwritten dead of House Viridane, whose names survive only in thorn and blood; each summoning must speak the name aloud.
* **Oken Florae ("The Grove-Litany"):** Carries ancestors in pocket-sized splinter-grafts, the only Animist variant that can practice inside a city.

**Fatal Flaw / Crisis:** Channelling 15+ Resonance causes physical collapse: fire burns twice as hot against their skin, conventional medical healing fails, and demanding spirits tear 1d6 Wyrd damage from their marrow every turn. Today, the three ancestral dialects are drifting out of alignment; young Animists trying to speak all three simultaneously suffer catastrophic brain hemorrhages.

**Founder(s):** Kael the Herd-Runner, Nyssa the Herbalist, and Theron the Scholar—all long dead. Current leader: Convenor Sera Three-Scars, the only surviving practitioner capable of holding all three ancestral dialects in her throat at once, a feat that is actively rotting her lungs.

# APEX

**Origin:** Founded during the early decades of the freeze by Sylas, a frontier scout who sliced open his own eardrums to track invisible predators through the freezing fog. By surrendering his hearing, Sylas trained his nerves to feel the ground shudder when a beast took a breath fifty paces away. He hunted a mist-stalker through the ironwood for seven days, matching its breathing until he drove a shadow-glaive through its heart.

**Identity:** A ruthless pack hunter who pairs a heavy bladed glaive with a trained war beast. The Apex fights without wasted movement, slipping through enemy flanks while their hunting beast tears open throats. Through the Mark system, the Apex marks targets for coordinated pack strikes—building lethal momentum not through brute force, but through relentless flanking pressure.

**Role in World:** The primary vanguard against wilderness horrors. Frontier logging outposts and wooden wall garrisons hire Apex rangers to track maneaters and scout through blinding blizzards. They communicate strictly through hand signs and weapon taps, rarely speaking or entering heated taverns.

**Cultural Affiliation:** Practiced by the wood-carving Mimir and northern Skald rangers.

*Eight Cultural Variants:*
* **Masked Mimir ("The Mask-Hunter"):** Uses a carved wooden face-mask to anchor their sanity while their senses range miles through the mist.
* **Woven Mimir ("The Sentinel-Tracker"):** Treats every patrol as a live combat watch along the ironwood palisades.
* **Unwoven Mimir ("The Fog-Walker"):** Dissolves their physical silhouette into cold vapor to ambush beasts from inside the mist.
* **Skald ("The Glacier-Stalker"):** Reads cracked glacier ice like a book, tracking beast weight across packed snowdrifts.
* **Clean Vreken ("The Glow-Trailer"):** Follows glowing fungal trails along subterranean root networks.
* **Marked Vreken ("The Mycelium-Scent"):** Uses fungal filaments beneath their skin as a physical tripwire network to feel prey moving overhead.
* **Ordan ("The Steppe-Scent"):** Tracks game across open grasslands by reading crushed steppe-grass and thermal wind currents.
* **Waste-Solari ("The Ash-Stalker"):** Reads footfalls in scoria and heat-memory in lava-glass, running an ash-hound at heel; the trails keep ending at sealed vents that are warm again.

**Fatal Flaw / Crisis:** The freezing fog has begun acting with predatory intelligence. Beast packs are actively erasing their tracks, and Unwoven scouts who dissolve into the vapor are vanishing entirely. Sylas has realized that the fog-horrors are no longer fleeing his hunters—they are hunting him.

**Founder(s):** Sylas the Silent—alive, completely deaf, and commanding the Silent Hunt through hand-signals from deep inside the ironwood forest. Notable figure: Maeve of the Canopy, who led the first column of civilian refugees through the memory-eating fog.

**Sites:** The Still Blind (the Greythorn oath-ring, founded the week Sylas came back deaf), Sylas's Clearing (the Ironwood Heart training grove of the Silent Sisterhood), the Quiet Hollow (where the sensory Trade is actually struck, and something that is not the fog takes its share), and the Weeping Shelf (a Hunger-Glacier record-room thawing open onto older, fresher tracks). The Trade itself is a bargain with **the Quiet**, the Old God of absence.

# ARCANONEER

**Origin:** Four centuries ago the Nethien archivist Valerius proved that elemental magic had always been a grammar, not a gift: every combination is a sentence, and the world enforces it the way Morvane enforces a contract. He drafted the Grand Nomenclature, the first legal syntax of the eight frequencies, and filed weaves as documents submitted for silent approval. When the Fexric began treating the same grammar as engineering in the Underground Proving Grounds, and the Stargazer Astril reached it independently by charting the frequencies as starlight cycles, the discipline stopped being one people's secret. Valerius did not die. His name became a clause in the Heart-Vault, and his crystal prism still rests in a cradle of root-glass in Atropolis, neither alive nor dead.

**Identity:** A cold contract-sniper whose spells are legal decrees channeled through a crystal focus, scroll, or memory-glass lens. Arcanoneers do not cast from emotion or faith; they dictate terms. Each elemental sphere is a clause, each weave a document, and Morvane rejects internally inconsistent weaves with severe prejudice. The discipline offers ruinous combination breadth in exchange for one brutal constraint: a Nethien Arcanoneer who contradicts themselves may void every contract they have ever filed.

**Role in World:** The ruling jurists and artillery elite of Atropolis. Arcanoneers sit at the heads of high courts, arbitrate maritime trade contracts at deep-water docks, and break siege lines during fortress wars, though the Clockwork Fexric guilds now license weaves like machinery and the Caustic Fexric sell combinations no guild will certify.

**Cultural Affiliation:** The Nethien (contract-syntax), the Clockwork Fexric (guild engineering), the Caustic Fexric (salvage improvisation), and the Stargazer Astril (starlight cycles).

*Four Lineage Variants:*
* **Nethien ("The Contract-Weaver"):** Every sphere is a clause and every weave is filed; consistency is survival, because a contradiction may void every contract they have ever filed.
* **Clockwork Fexric ("The Gear-Weaver"):** Guild-certified combinations only, executed through regulators and thermal sinks; flawless within tolerances, incapable of improvisation.
* **Caustic Fexric ("The Scrap-Weaver"):** Jury-rigged catalysts, no certified matrix, a twenty-five percent backlash rate, and combinations the guild has never seen.
* **Stargazer Astril ("The Star-Charted Weaver"):** The eight frequencies mapped as starlight cycles; weaves resolve as celestial mechanics, and a mistimed transit fades rather than detonating.

**Fatal Flaw / Crisis:** The Canopy-Ledger fracture. The institution that underwrites the craft is splitting between magical warfare and dissolution, and if half the Ledger dissolves its oaths, the Nethien who filed those oaths may legally cease to exist; Morvane does not distinguish between a caster and their filings. Meanwhile the Caustic Scrap-Weavers are being treated as saviors for the first time, which worries them more than the guild ever did.

**Founder(s):** Valerius the Scriptor—ascended into the First Contract, a clause in the Heart-Vault, neither alive nor dead. His crystal prism is preserved in Atropolis. Current leader: Ledger-Prime Vel-Otharen, whose flesh has crystallized into seventy percent solid mana-glass. Notable figure: Kaelen the Unbroken, who held the harbor docks against an assault of sea-serpents.

**Sites:** The Canopy-Ledger (the Atropolis seat, paralyzed by the factional dispute), the Heart-Vault (where the First Contract grows through the bark and the Nomenclature's original syntax is still consulted), the Underground Proving Grounds (Gearworks Gulch, where the Gear-Weaver/Scrap-Weaver schism happened at one bench), and the Quiet Observatory (Sundrift Vale, where the Stargazer chart-keepers have opened a ninth column for a star that should not be there).

# AUGUR

**Origin:** Founded during the first freezing eclipse by Cassia, a mountain star-watcher whose bronze telescopes frosted over when the sun went dark. Desperate for answers, she butchered a sacrificial glacier-elk with a scaling knife and spread its steaming intestines across the ice. Reading the purple veins of the liver, she predicted the exact minute the temperature would drop thirty degrees. The temporal feedback burned away her memories, leaving her blind to her own past.

**Identity:** A combat prophet who reads the next ten seconds of bloodshed in fresh gore and splintered bone. Augurs do not predict grand royal destinies; they tell a warrior which way an enemy axe will swing, which stone will give way, and where an arrow will land. Every combat prophecy exacts a sharp toll in physical stamina or mental clarity.

**Role in World:** High-ranking tactical advisors in mountain fortress archives and war rooms. Generals use Augurs to position shield walls before an ambush springs, though common soldiers avoid sharing mess halls with them due to their blood-stained knives and unsettling muttering.

**Cultural Affiliation:** Practiced by Skald mountain-haruspexes, Astril crystalline seers, Vreken peat-bog diviners, Veldun web-tallies, Hollow-Solari flame-watchers, and the deep-preservation readers of the Myrathil.

*Eleven Cultural Variants:*
* **Skald ("The Glacier-Haruspex"):** Cassia’s original line. Slits open arctic beasts to read tactical movements in steaming viscera before the frost sets in.
* **Vashir Astril ("The Star-Viscera Reader"):** Reads future fractures through the glowing cracks in their own crystalline skin.
* **Silath Astril ("The Suppressed Oracle"):** Forces prophetic screams from suppressed alien memories locked inside their bone marrow.
* **Tessen ("The Keep-Prophet"):** Predicts military breaches by reading stress fractures spreading through stone fortress walls.
* **Clean Vreken ("The Bog-Gore Diviner"):** Pulls preserved corpses from peat bogs to extract historical warnings from ancient dead tissue.
* **Marked Vreken ("The Mycelium-Haruspex"):** Reads incoming danger through the flashing light pulses of subterranean root mushrooms.
* **Veldun ("The Web-Tally"):** Reads the strain-patterns of the obligation-web; the web and the Reckoner's ledger are both coming up short.
* **Hollow-Solari ("The Sol's Breath-Reader"):** Reads the dying star's flicker-patterns in wordless vigil; a four-century ledger of dimming that no one outside the Deep-Vault has been told.
* **Deepling Myrathil ("The Trench-Haruspex"):** Reads drowned elders preserved in abyssal cold and pressure; the longest reading window in the tradition.
* **Shoreling Myrathil ("The Tide-Reader"):** Reads the future in what the sea returns to the tide-line, and in what order it surrenders it.
* **Riverling Myrathil ("The Current-Haruspex"):** Reads the standing-wave patterns of river confluences; works far inland, and drowns in noise when the rivers run wrong.

**Fatal Flaw / Crisis:** Prophetic accuracy has plummeted from 93% to 41% across the continent. Entrails are showing impossible, contradictory futures. Something in the upper atmosphere is warping the flow of time, creating timeline echoes that are causing seers to choke on conflicting visions.

**Sites:** Cassia's Terrace (the frost-mirror origin site above the Frozen Archive, where the elk-vein pattern is kept in ice), the Entrail Chambers (the reading chambers below it), the Contradiction Vault (the lead-lined tally of failed auguries, now short by an unknown quantity), and the Last Cut (the Black Fen peat-cut where the bog stopped returning its dead). The Augur's ledger is tallied by **the Reckoner**, the Old God of even and odd.

**Founder(s):** Cassia the Star-Eyed—her ancient body sits preserved upright inside a glacier vault, eyes frozen wide open as she whispers predictions to scribes. Current leader: Archive-Mistress Skadi Glass-Eye, who takes dictation from a founder who no longer remembers her own name.

# BERSERKER

**Origin:** Founded during the great southern migration by Grum the Smith, who led his freezing clan into the volcanic caldera of Sundale. When an armored ice-wyrm smashed through their stone barricade, Grum dropped his smithing hammer, inhaled the sulfurous smoke from a magma fissure, and charged. His veins boiled with so much adrenaline that his bicep tendons snapped, yet he crushed the monster's skull with his bare fists.

**Identity:** A walking engine of biological destruction. Berserkers do not wield spells; they force their own anatomy past human limits through boiling rage and chemical fury. This physical state is the Rage—muscles tear themselves from bone to strike with bone-crushing force, while the heart races like a runaway steam boiler.

**Role in World:** Frontline shock troops and heavy tunnel-breakers. Mercenary companies hire Berserkers to smash through reinforced iron gates and clear cave-in rubble, though frontier taverns ban them on sight due to their lethal barroom brawls.

**Cultural Affiliation:** Practiced by Skald mountain-clans (Hunger-Pact Sworn), Waste-Solari forge-clans (Caldera-Forged), and vat-grown Morgh Groven laborers (Vat-Woken).

*Three Cultural Variants:*
* **Skald ("The Hunger-Pact Sworn"):** Inherits ancestral fury from survivors who ate their fallen comrades during the Hunger Winter to keep the bloodline alive.
* **Waste-Solari ("The Caldera-Forged"):** Draws Rage from geothermal resonance instead of ancestry, fighting as if carrying on a personal fistfight with the volcano.
* **Morgh Groven ("The Vat-Woken"):** Engineered for docility, these heavy clay-clones weaponize dormant growth chemicals in their veins to tear off their own shock-collars.

**Fatal Flaw / Crisis:** While in Rage, Berserkers ignore all incoming medical healing. Pushing past 100 Heat triggers total metabolic burnout, causing heart valves and lungs to seize. In the mountains, a new generation of berserkers is igniting Rage without performing the ancestral rituals, sparking a bloody civil war among clan elders.

**Founder(s):** Grum Bloodhammer—dead for centuries, his colossal smithing hammer rests beside an active lava vent in Sundale. Current leader: Blood-Priest Hark Ash-Hammer, who guards the ancient anvil and orders the execution of any warrior who loses control of the fire.

**Sites:** The Harath-Vault Arenas (the geothermal proving-grounds where a Berserker is not blooded until they have won a match), the Forge of Grum (the original forge, still lit, tended by one silent keeper), the Long Table (the Bloodhammer Sump's basalt origin-table of the Hunger Pact, set for an Unbound guest no one will seat), the Unbound Deep (the heretical settlement under Emberspire, where Berserkers who ignite without the Pact live under sentence), and the Collection Sumps (the Deep Alchemist annex where vanished Vat-Woken are taken for audit).

# CHRONARCH

**Origin:** Three centuries ago, an engineer named Nesta realized that stone braces could not stop a moving glacier from crushing her subterranean holdfast. In desperation, she bolted a brass clockwork gear-engine directly into her sternum and linked its mainspring to her heartbeat. By forcing her own timeline into a localized loop, she stalled the falling ice for three days until her clan evacuated, permanently trapping her body in chronological friction.

**Identity:** A living temporal anchor whose spells stitch broken timelines back together. Chronarchs carry brass hourglasses not as ornaments, but as vital gauges measuring the heavy metabolic cost of their craft. When they rewind a companion’s bleeding chest wound, the kinetic recoil etches deep bruises into the Chronarch's own ribs.

**Role in World:** Emergency siege engineers and field medics. Mining guilds pay fortunes for Chronarchs to freeze falling mine shafts and reverse explosive cave-ins, though church inquisitors hunt them down for delaying the natural death of the sun.

**Cultural Affiliation:** Practiced by guild-licensed Fexric tinkerers and long-limbed Groven bridge-engineers.

*Three Cultural Variants:*
* **Kethrin Fexrick ("The Gear-Stitcher"):** Treats time as a mechanical gear-train with swappable teeth, maintaining their chest-engines with oil cans and spanners.
* **Ithran Groven ("The Bone-Calibrator"):** Uses their extended clay-bone limbs as physical lightning rods to ground temporal shockwaves across wide areas.
* **Tessen ("The Keep-Anchor"):** Anchors their life force to old fortress foundations, freezing time inside castle walls while the outer world ages.

**Fatal Flaw / Crisis:** Chronarchs suffer permanent 50% vulnerability to Arcane and Blight energy. Forced movement drops their evasion to zero and causes temporal backlash. Worse, Nesta is actively disappearing from historical records—written books bearing her signature are going blank, threatening to erase all living Chronarchs in a single temporal paradox.

**Founder(s):** Nesta the Clockwork Engineer—vanishing from physical reality. Current leader: Conclave-Prime Fex-Vestara, an engineer racing against the clock to build a replacement master-engine before Nesta fades completely from history.

**Sites:** The Chronostasis Chamber (Nesta's engine, her chair empty three weeks), the Stalled Fall (the glacier wall she stopped for three days, now dripping once a day), the First Turbine Gallery (the quarantined oldest machine, where time stutters and the seal is broken from the inside each season), and the Slow Vault (a Tessen undercroft whose collapse has been arriving for two hundred years, three stones closer this winter). The failing turbine is the stirring of **Vurath**, the Underchorus.

# CRUSADER

**Origin:** Founded during the first decade of the freeze by Lord-Captain Vane of House Solvan, who refused to abandon the border keeps when the sun grew cold. He stripped the gold trim from his family armor, welded raw, glowing celestial shards into his breastplate, and led a vanguard into the blizzard to hunt down winter abominations. His radiant fervor burned so hot that frost melted from his greatsword mid-swing.

**Identity:** A heavy-armored juggernaut fueled by the dying warmth of the celestial sacrifice. Crusaders do not hide behind fragile wards; they plant heavy iron boots in the snow, raise two-handed greatswords, and build **Fervor** with every crushing blow. When their fervor peaks, they discharge blazing starlight waves that shatter enemy plate armor and burn Wyrd horrors to ash.

**Role in World:** The armored frontline fist of the Dawn Vigil. Crusaders spearhead expeditions into infested ruins, hold bridgeheads against swarming abominations, and execute corrupted nobles. While respected for their iron discipline, common folk fear their unyielding zeal and quickness to condemn suspects to the pyre.

**Cultural Affiliation:** Practiced by Skald heavy champions (Frost-Hearth Zealots who treat the greatsword as a mobile hearth), Waste-Solari forge-knights (Magma Crusaders who supplement geothermal defense with starlight), and Brutish Astril (Crystal Judgments who refract Aex's song through their crystalline skin).

*Three Specialization Paths:*
* **Solvan Executioner:** Frontline shock breaker wielding colossal greatswords. Converts Fervor into armor-melting smites and creates permanent zones of consecrated fire.
* **Dawn Bastion:** An immovable shield-bearer who channels starlight into heavy tower plates, absorbing hits for allies and raising glowing barricades against missile fire.
* **Harmonic Inquisitor:** A specialized anti-mage who uses starlight lightning like a tuning fork, interrupting enemy spellcasts and shattering magical wards.

**Fatal Flaw / Crisis:** Crusaders depend entirely on continuous melee momentum. If a Crusader is bogged down or separated from the front line, their Fervor dissipates rapidly, leaving their heavy plate vulnerable to flanking strikes and kinetic artillery.

**Founder(s):** Lord-Captain Vane the Unbroken—fell at the Wyrd-Breach, his shattered starlight blade mounted above the Great Forge in the Harath-Vault. Current leader: Hierophant Aethelgard, an unyielding commander who demands total obedience and views all who question the Dawn Vigil as traitors.

**Sites:** The Obsidian Citadels (the Vigil's toll-fortresses along the Ashen Escarpment, where the gates keep refugees in and the Wyrd out), the Mounted Blade (Vane's shattered sword above the Great Forge, dimming since the vote to reforge), the Glass Field (four miles of escarpment vitrified by a century of Solvan Judgments), and the Reforging Altar (near Emberspire, where six true Monolith fragments wait with a seventh that hums half a tone off).

# FALSE PROPHET

**Origin:** Founded two centuries ago by Li Wei, a nomadic horse-herder who tracked a falling meteor into an impact crater. Instead of iron ore, he uncovered a cracked black monolith humming with an empty psychic vacuum. When he stared into the dark crystal where the sun's reflection should have been, his mind shattered under the voices of dead stars. He climbed out of the crater with milk-white eyes, preaching the gospel of the cosmic silence.

**Identity:** A psychic instigator who channels the cold vacuum between stars. False Prophets do not pray; they tear open psychic channels into the minds of others. Accumulating **Madness** through apocalyptic sermons and mental assaults, their spells grow increasingly devastating as their own mental stability crumbles toward total collapse.

**Role in World:** Charismatic cult leaders and underground revolutionaries. Hunted by city watches and burned as heretics by church knights, False Prophets operate in squalid refugee camps and mining slums, building devoted followings among starving workers by promising that the cold universe will swallow their masters whole.

**Cultural Affiliation:** Practiced by Stargazer Astril (False Stars whose constructed faith borrows the credibility of genuine Lumia resonance), Brutish Astril (Gagged Evangelists who preach against their own suppressed heritage), Tessen keep-dwellers (Keep-Prophets with captive congregations), Ordan herd-watchers (Thinned-Herd Preachers who preach into the silence where the ancestor-songs failed), and Vreken debtors (Debt-Preachers who mint absolution from belief).

*Five Cultural Variants:*
* **Stargazer Astril ("The False Star"):** Borrows the credibility of a real fragment of a dead world's biosphere; the congregation cannot tell the prophet from the temple.
* **Brutish Astril ("The Gagged Evangelist"):** Harnesses the rage of a suppressed heritage; genuine supernatural resistance reads as sacred conviction.
* **Tessen ("The Keep-Prophet"):** Builds Madness faster than any variant because the congregation is physically sealed in a dying keep with no other source of meaning.
* **Ordan ("The Thinned-Herd Preacher"):** Preaches into the silence where the ancestor-songs failed; the sermon rides the wind and belief travels with the migration.
* **Vreken ("The Debt-Preacher"):** Sells absolution from debts that outlive the debtor, minting belief as payment against a ledger the Keeper has never audited.

**Fatal Flaw / Crisis:** Accumulating 20 Madness points triggers a violent psychic convulsion, stunning the Prophet. In recent months, the voice in their heads has begun giving precise, chilling orders: *"March to the mountain vaults. Break the iron seals. Let the cold inside."*

**Founder(s):** Li Wei the Blind—alive, bedridden, and continuously whispering cosmic coordinates. Current leader: Cell-Mother Mor-Vereth, a former slum-debtor who manages the secret underground network of preaching cells around Li Wei’s fevered breathing.

**Sites:** The Crater of the First Silence (the Sundrift impact site where Li Wei found the hum), the Cathedral of the Last Light (an inverted ship's hull above the peat-bog, its services held in absolute darkness), the Breathing Cell (Li Wei's hidden sickroom under the Over-Shanty, with Mor-Vereth's ledger of the dead beneath his cot), and the Lowest Vault (the sealed under-vault of the Frozen Archive the Voice orders opened, which the Archivists deny exists).

# GAMBIT

**Origin:** The Gambler is a hazard-pact archetype older than any kingdom: a mortal at the exact point of irreversible catastrophe, extended one line of credit by an entity the theologians of Nordhalla catalogue as Mael-Zhul, and known to those bound to its ledger only as **the House**. The House demands no temples and no prayers. It feeds on the electric spike of mortal panic right before the dice settle, and it stakes its debtors with the power to twist the Wyrd—bending probability, warping consequence, and turning parlor games into lethal metaphysical mandates. The collateral is the soul. Win the initiation Death Roll and Wyrd surges through the veins like boiling ice; lose, and the thread snaps where you sit. The modern Gambit fused two burdens when Jax, a Merryn captain, rolled dice against a storm-spirit at Merrowport and lost his blood's warmth, and Lyra, a Veldun probability-watcher, severed alternate timelines to save her caravan on the Cragjaw approach and shattered her own consciousness doing it.

**Identity:** A probability surgeon who trades in stolen luck and severed fate-lines. Gambits do not play cards for tavern coin—they gamble for their next heartbeat. Fortune is the House's credit drawn against their own future; Karmic Debt is the marker coming due. They calibrate constantly, betting on which icicle snaps, which patron throws the first punch, because to a Gambit those small games are the barometer that tells them whether the House is smiling or preparing to collect.

**Role in World:** High-stakes trade brokers, maritime navigators, and underworld kingpins, but never guildmasters—Gamblers recognize no guilds, only rival debtors working the same cosmic margins. They drift to the **Dead Pots**, the liminal gambling dens hidden in rotting hulls, dry mine shafts, and backrooms behind fishmarkets, where steel stays sheathed by universal agreement and disputes settle on the felt. The loudest of them sits in Oakhaven, on the Nordhalla coast. Coin means little in a Dead Pot; they trade in weighted dice cut from sea-horror teeth, blank bone tiles, loadstone coins, and bad paper signed by dead men that still carries resonance.

**Cultural Affiliation:** Three heritages carry the archetype, each cornered by doom and each carrying the curse of its first wager: the Merryn of the Iceheart Sea, the Veldun of the Bryngloom, and the Caustic Fexric of the Cragjaw sumps.

*Three Cultural Variants:*

**Origin:** Founded during the Celestial Star-Fall by Damon, a forge-smith who raised a heavy alchemical iron shield to block a solar flare during the entombment of the infant sun. The blast fused the iron to his forearms and burned glowing starlight veins into his chest. Rather than dying, he treated the radiation as an engineering challenge: designing shields with grounding rods to absorb and dismantle incoming spells.

**Identity:** A heavy-armored anti-magic bulwark who intercepts spells meant for the party. Spellguards do not cast sparkling magic; they drag raw lightning and fireballs directly into their heavy shields, grounding the voltage through their own marrow. Absorbing spells generates **Silence Resonance**, which can be vented in kinetic shockwaves, but holding too much radiation cooks their internal organs.

**Role in World:** Invaluable frontline anchors for any adventuring party or military column. Every expedition into magical ruins demands at least one Spellguard to defuse arcane traps and block incoming dragon-breath.

**Cultural Affiliation:** Practiced by Nethien jurists, Thalren sappers, and the Solari of both bloodlines.

*Four Cultural Variants:*
* **Nethien ("The Clause-Canceller"):** Incoming fireballs are breaches of contract; the annulment is filed before impact, and the spell dissolves on procedural grounds.
* **Thalren ("The Wyrd-Defuser"):** Disarms magical traps like a sapper, disconnecting trigger from payload, and always expecting the second trigger.
* **Hollow-Solari ("The Silent-Guard"):** Intercepts from Vault-Breath stillness, a calm so absolute that incoming magic falls into them rather than lands; the deep-vault tomb-guards.
* **Waste-Solari ("The Forge-Shield"):** Badland rangers who take spells on the angle, redirecting fire the way they redirect a vent-rupture, and letting the terrain absorb what they cannot.

**Fatal Flaw / Crisis:** Spellguards suffer +50% vulnerability to physical bludgeoning damage. Holding unspent Silence Resonance causes **Arcane Saturation**, dealing Blight burn damage to the Spellguard and pulsing radioactive shockwaves into nearby companions.

**Founder(s):** Damon the Iron-Handed—dead, his fused shield preserved in a furnace shrine. Current leader: Bulwark-Captain Thrak-Damos, whose forearms glow with blue radiation scars through his heavy wool tunic.

**Sites:** The Deflection Halls (the lead-lined training and detox chambers beneath Greymark Keep, running at maximum capacity), the Iron-Handed Shrine (Damon's slagged shield above the Harath-Vault, where the Keepers have stopped reading the First Rule aloud), and the Dimming Yards (the lead-walled cooling cells in Vulkars' Karst where saturated Spellguards dim below a lamp-line, and the bells that never stop glowing).

# TOXICOLOGIST

**Origin:** Founded during the early freezing decades by Varis, a frontier medic who needed a way to defend his scout patrols from giant mist-predators. Realizing that steel swords bounced off chitinous beast-hides, he began milking venom from dead horrors and concentrating the bile over coal braziers. Decades of boiling acidic secretions destroyed his sense of taste, stained his fingernails purple, and left him with severe chronic hand tremors—yet he can still measure a lethal drop of venom without spilling.

**Identity:** A self-poisoned combat chemist who weaponizes lethal toxins, gas bombs, and flesh-rotting catalysts. Toxicologists do not use clean healing magic; they turn their own blood into a toxic solvent. Every poison vial thrown costs chemical reagents and physical vitality, turning the surrounding ground into smoking acid pools.

**Role in World:** Frontier monster-slayers and black-market alchemists. Forestry companies hire Toxicologists to clear predator nests with toxic smoke, though tavern owners refuse to serve them drinks in civilian glassware.

**Cultural Affiliation:** Practiced by Thalren frontier scouts, Tethered Mimir brewers, the Withered of the Deep-Quarter, and Florae thorn-gatherers.

*Five Cultural Variants:*
* **Thalren ("The Fog-Distiller"):** Varis’s original line. Distills long-lasting area-denial poisons that linger in damp hollows for weeks.
* **Tethered Mimir ("The Floor-Brewer"):** Harvests decomposing tree bark to make slow-acting paralytic oils.
* **Withered ("The Silence-Distiller"):** Distills from their own decaying flesh; null-distillates that register as nothing on the First Contract.
* **Viridian Florae ("The Thorn-Venom"):** Distills toxins directly from their own sap-blood, creating poisons tuned to their specific lineage.
* **Oken Florae ("The Hidden-Cuil"):** Operates in secret within human cities, engineering untraceable poisons for political assassinations.

**Fatal Flaw / Crisis:** All healing received by a Toxicologist is permanently halved. Fire damage ignites the chemical residue on their clothes, causing their own vials to detonate. In the northern woods, the freezing fog is altering the chemical properties of venom, causing standard antidotes to spoil into lethal neurotoxins.

**Founder(s):** Varis the Trembling—ancient, hands shaking, still working in his canopy laboratory. He views the changing fog chemistry as a personal insult and refuses to leave his distillation coils until he has solved the new formula.

**Sites:** The Distillery (the canopy laboratory above the Shallows where the newest vials are labeled with question marks), Thornwood Grove (the unmarked Third Harvest site where apprentice Shakers are shown what the mist does to the unprepared), the Peat-Crypts (the flooded Vreken vaults beneath Over-Shanty, brewing unlicensed reagents as the patrols double), the Deep-Quarter Null-Laboratory (the lead-sheathed peat-hold beneath the Withered rope-slum, losing containment to the deepening Silence), and Nerath's Ironwood Deep Laboratory (a single unmarked room in a dead ironwood's roots, where a schismatic synthesizes poisons for clients who have lost faith in Varis).

# WARDEN

**Origin:** Founded during the great underground riots by Alaric, a mine guard stationed in the deep tunnels. When laboratory cages shattered and mutated cave-beasts slaughtered his squad, Alaric drove a heavy iron hauling chain through his own forearm, wrapped the links around the monster's throat, and spiked the chain into the granite wall with his own body as the pin. He held the beast for three days until it suffocated. When rescuers offered to cut the rusted chain out of his arm, he refused.

**Identity:** A heavy-armored lockdown specialist who pins colossal beasts in place using heavy iron chains grafted directly into forearm bones and vertebrae. The Warden does not cast nature spells; they throw massive barbed chains around a monster’s neck, locking their boots into the earth to hold the horror still while their companions hack it to pieces.

**Role in World:** The unbreakable backbone of mountain mining holds and frontier gates. Mining cartels pay double wages for a Warden to stand watch at tunnel heads, knowing that when a cave horror charges, the Warden will chain themselves to it rather than let it reach the miners.

**Cultural Affiliation:** Practiced by subterranean Morgh Groven, Fexric chain-smiths, and mountain Skald hunters.

*Seven Cultural Variants:*
* **Morgh Groven ("The Vat-Grounded"):** Invented the flesh-graft chain. Wearing the chains voluntarily is their ultimate declaration of freedom from the alchemists who engineered them.
* **Ithran Groven ("The Span-Tether"):** Uses bridge-engineering mathematics to anchor chains across multiple charging enemies simultaneously.
* **Drall Fexrick ("The Gear-Tension"):** Uses ratchet winches bolted to their armor to mechanically reel in hooked monsters.
* **Kethrin Fexrick ("The Guild-Jailer"):** Treats chaining as a regulated trade, using certified links with documented tensile strength.
* **Skald ("The Glacier-Chain"):** Uses cold-treated iron chains that shrink and bite deeper as the temperature drops.
* **Clean Vreken ("The Glow-Tether"):** Uses glowing chains to illuminate the position of invisible horrors in the dark.
* **Marked Vreken ("The Mycelium-Leash"):** Threads barbed chains through subterranean tree roots, anchoring beasts to bedrock miles away.

**Fatal Flaw / Crisis:** Extreme winter cold is making standard iron brittle, causing chains to snap under the weight of charging horrors. Fexric smiths have developed lighter chardalyn-alloy chains, but the metal whispers in the dark, slowly driving its wearers insane. Alaric refuses to let his disciples touch the whispering metal, even as broken-iron casualties mount.

**Founder(s):** Alaric the Law-Keeper—alive, his ancient Thrumm biology keeping him alive centuries past his time. The original chain remains rusted deep into his forearm bone. Notable figures: The Fexrick Drall Smiths, who perform the surgical flesh-grafting rites in mountain forge-clinics.

**Sites:** The Forge of Alaric (the original chain-forge at Frostmaw, still struggling to meet demand), the Chain-Hold (the order's seat and initiation hall, where the scar-map wall has begun leaving gaps), the Graft-Theatre (the surgical hall beneath the Frozen Archive, where chardalyn grafts are filed under the volunteers' own names), and the Whisper-Galleries (the deepest tunnels at Frostmaw, where the first chardalyn testers tether themselves to the walls and the bell-rope has rung twice all year).
