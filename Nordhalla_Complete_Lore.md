## PART I: THE CONTINENT

### Geography

Nordhalla is a **brutalist cathedral of frozen black fjords and towering glaciers** in the far north — **directly inspired by Icewind Dale.** A **separate continent** — no land borders with any other region. East across open sea lies Frostwood Reach. South across open water lies Sundale. Minor outskirt islands dot its continental shelf.

Three geographic forces shape the land:
- **Glaciers** — pressed in from the north and west, halted in place by the Glacier Bargain
- **Geothermal vents** — volcanic craters punching warmth through the permafrost
- **Black fjords** — deep, basalt-walled inlets carved by the pre-Binding landscape

### Climate

**Eternal winter.** The Glacier Bargain traded summer for halted glaciers. There is no spring. There is no summer. Only cold, with brief geothermal pockets of survival. Morð worsens each generation as the buried Solbrand's residual thermal resonance continues to fade.

### Government

Ruled by **King-Jarl Halvar Skalvyr, called Jarn-Tand ("Iron-Tooth")** of **House Skalvyr**. He unified the clans by force, built the **Sunder-Wall**, and established the **Icechamber Syndicate** trade monopoly. The **Cleansing of the Hearth** — his religious persecution of tribal Animists — is ongoing.

### The Three Pillars of Skalvyr Power

1. **The Frozen Archive** — institutional control of history and prophecy
2. **The Icechamber Syndicate** — economic control of all trade
3. **The Sunder-Wall** — military control of migration and movement

## PART II: THE BINDING (The Founding Sin)

### The Cosmology

Sol is a **sun deity** — a young god thriving in his cocoon, almost ready to be born. Deities are born inside stars. When a new deity is created, a sun is born (son). When a daughter is born, a moon is born. The dormant moon in the sky is a daughter deity — young, still developing.

**AEX** is Sol's mother. A celestial deity. **AETHIL** is Sol's father. Aex's partner. Together they are the parents of the youngest deity in the cosmos — and the one most vulnerable to predation.

### The Predator

**Keth-Amar** is an ancient predator. Part of a race that hunts deities before they're born. He has killed dozens of stars. He consumes the nascent god inside before it can fully form. Sol — thriving, close to birth — is exactly what attracts him.

### The Sacrifice

Aex senses Keth-Amar coming. She knows Sol will be consumed if she does nothing. She faces an impossible choice: break the unwritten rule, or lose her child.

**The unwritten rule:** Deities must not commune with mortals. To interact with mortals, a celestial being must permanently degrade themselves — strip their celestial status, become something less. Non-celestial. Mortal-grade. This is irreversible.

Aex breaks the rule. She strips her celestial status. Aethil does the same. They become something less than gods — but they can now speak to the houses of Mythrill.

### The Pleading

Aethil and Aex plead with the houses: **bind your lineage to Aex. Cast ancient magic. Seal Sol safely inside the earth's core.** Otherwise he will be lost forever — consumed by Keth-Amar.

The houses accept. Each is offered a gift based on what they wish for — magic to help them survive the frozen world that will result from Sol's hiding. The magic is celestial power shaped by mortal desire. It is volatile, powerful, and transformative.

### The Binding

**Aex sacrifices herself.** She binds herself to the core, wrapping around Sol, becoming the shell — the protective barrier between her son and the predator. Her body IS the binding. Her sacrifice is the wall.

Sol is sealed inside the earth's core. The world goes dark. No sun. No warmth from the sky. Only heat radiating from the earth. Glaciers grow. The frozen age begins.

### The Second Deal — Keth-Amar's Trick (The Double-Deception)

**Phase 1 — The Predator Arrives:**

Keth-Amar arrives. He can still smell Sol. He spreads his Wyrd onto Mythrill — saturating the world, learning everything. He discovers what each house wants. What problems they face. What they're struggling with. He learns their weaknesses.

**Phase 2 — The Disguise:**

Keth-Amar disguises himself as Aethil. He speaks in her voice. He tells the houses: "Sol is ready to be freed. The binding has served its purpose. Release him, and the world will be warm again. The glaciers will melt. Summer will return."

All six houses believe the trick. They're desperate. The world is freezing. They want relief. They want their gift to mean something. They want to save the world.

**Phase 3 — Viridane's Refusal:**

House Viridane doesn't believe the voice. They didn't ask for anything. They didn't want change. They confer with the fae — the spirits of the Frostwood, the old ones who remember what came before.

The fae sense something is amiss. They tell Viridane: this is not Aethil. This is the predator.

Viridane realizes the truth: they've been living WITH nature, not against it. The gift of Primal wasn't a curse — it was a calling. The roots grow into you because you ARE the forest. They weren't meant to control nature. They were meant to BE nature.

They refuse the deal. They walk away from the table.

**Phase 4 — The Transformation:**

But Keth-Amar's Wyrd is everywhere. If Viridane stays as they are — human, identifiable, traceable — he will find them. He will hunt them through knowledge, through memory, through the threads of who they are.

The fae offer a solution: **change everything.** Not just their names. Not just their records. Their entire species. Their bodies. Their forms. Their very nature.

The fae reshape House Viridane into **treant humanoids** — the Florae. Root-bound, bark-skinned, connected to the forest in ways humans never were. They become something Keth-Amar cannot recognize. Something his Wyrd cannot track. Because they are no longer human. They are no longer a house. They are the forest itself.

Their entire history is erased. Every record. Every name. Every memory. The other houses help with this — but the fae ensure it is complete. Even the Florae forget who they were. They forget they were ever Viridane. They forget they ever made a deal.

**Phase 5 — The Houses' Counter-Trick:**

The other houses hear nothing from Viridane. They figure out Keth-Amar is lying. But they can't refuse without revealing they know.

So they **substitute House Morrath.** They tell Keth-Amar: "Morrath agrees. The seventh signature is sealed."

Keth-Amar thinks he's won. All seven houses agreed. The binding is his.

**But Morrath is a substitute.** Their monolith is false. Their signature is a lie. The binding cracks — but doesn't shatter. Because Viridane's pact still holds, and Morrath's signature is hollow.

**The Result:**

- Keth-Amar believes all seven houses agreed
- The binding cracks — Aex's shell fractures
- Keth-Amar can now feed slowly through the cracks
- But the binding doesn't shatter — because Viridane's pact still holds
- The Florae are hidden, forgotten, their magic fading
- House Morrath is elevated as the seventh house — a substitute, a hollow echo
- Three centuries of erasure follow — every trace of Viridane is removed

**The Silent Seventh:**

The official record lists seven houses: Thalreth, Skalvyr, Tesshan, Solvan, Mereval, Ordavan, and Morrath. All seven sealed the Binding Compact.

The truth: the original seventh was Viridane. They refused. They were transformed. They were erased. House Morrath was elevated as a substitute. The seventh monolith doesn't scream with a silenced refusal. It screams with a substituted name — a name that was never meant to be there.

## PART III: THE FROST-TITHE (Absolute)

Morð is the metaphysical cost on the Glacier Bargain, collected every frost-touched generation. Every Skald birth must "pay" — the infant survives by drawing the mother's warmth into itself, the bargain's metaphysical claim. The mother dies. The child survives, marked.

**The base rate is absolute: 1 in 7 mothers die in childbirth.** Nothing changes this number. No ritual, no artifact, no intervention moves the rate. The Skald have tried for eight centuries. The Astril have studied it. The Doom-Choir has calculated it. The number is the number.

### The Marked Children

Children who survive Morð are **Rime-Touched** — not a separate race, but a status. Blue-skinned, supernatural cold in their blood, breath freezes even in southern heat. They are the children the bargain collects. They are what Keth-Amar wanted from the start.

### The Rime-Touched

A Rime-Touched Skald carries:
- Blue skin
- Frost-touched hands (immune to burns)
- Supernatural cold in the blood
- Slow aging
- Vision issues in warmth

They work the forges of the Bloodhammer Sump. They serve as Berserkers. They are the visible price of Nordhalla's survival.

### The Culture's Response

The Skald have made peace with the rate. **Elaborate death-mourning rituals** are part of every birth — half the joy, half the funeral, every time. The Rime-Touched are simply accepted. The Cradle-Camp is half-hospice, half-ritual-ground. The Berserkers who staff it are not there to prevent death. They are there to make the death meaningful when it comes.

## PART IV: THE BERSERKER SACRIFICE

### The Class

A **Berserker** is a Rime-Touched warrior who has learned to channel Morð's pull through **Blóð** (the fury that ignites them in battle). Blóð is the same channel Morð uses to claim mothers at birth. By learning to wield that channel, a Berserker can intervene at the moment of Morð.

**There is no surviving the intervention.** A Berserker can save a mother. The Berserker will die.

### The Ritual

When Morð comes at a difficult birth:
- The Berserker kneels at the birthing mother's side
- They place both palms flat against her heart
- They **transfer their own ember** — the Rime-Touched fire that is the Berserker's gift — directly into the bargain
- The bargain accepts the Berserker's ember in place of the mother's life
- The mother lives. The child is born. The Berserker **dies shortly after.**

**Nothing can save the Berserker in this state.** It is the ultimate sacrifice.

### The Ember Passes

"Transfering the blood of the berserker" means the bargain claims the Berserker's flame, and the child is born with a fragment of the ember in their veins. **The child inherits the ember.** Each Berserker's sacrifice creates the next generation of Berserkers. The class perpetuates itself through death.

### The Cost of the Class

- Berserkers die younger
- Berserkers cannot easily have children (Morð is a cruel inheritance)
- The Blood-Heat, once ignited, can spread — berserker rages can become self-consuming
- A Berserker who absorbs too many Morð becomes a **Coldfire** — a being of living contradiction, half flame, half glacier

### The Cradle-Camp

The **Morð Cradle-Camp** is staffed by Berserkers who have volunteered for the duty. They sit with mothers in labor. They read the signs of an incoming Morð. They know that if they intervene, they will not leave the camp alive. They do it anyway.

The camp is part-birthing-ground, part-funeral-pyre, part-religious-ritual. The Rime-Touched children born there grow up knowing that their birth cost a Berserker's life, and that one day they may be the Berserker at someone else's birth.

### Grum's Founding

**Grum Bloodhammer**, founder of the Berserker tradition, was a Skald smith who had witnessed too many mothers die at his forge. He discovered Blóð while trying to keep a mother alive during a difficult birth. The forge-fire and the Morð-cold met in his blood, and the Berserker class was born from that single act of stubborn refusal to accept the bargain's arithmetic.

## PART V: SVÁLGHJARTA AND THE HUNGRÍD

### The Artifact — Sválghjarta (The Swallow-Heart)

When Keth-Amar tore pieces from Aex, he didn't just take tissue. He infused each piece with **Wyrd** — the demonic substance of the world. The corruption. Aex was pure. The pieces are not. Each artifact is a piece of a pure entity, wrapped in Wyrd corruption. The darkness isn't Aex's nature — it's Keth-Amar's fingerprint.

**Sválghjarta** is Aex's heart, frozen in a ring of ice deep beneath the northern glacier. It doesn't beat — it hungers. A void that can never be filled. The heart doesn't need to eat. It is simply insatiable. It feeds on **potential** — the road not taken, the power never realized, the futures it devours. It is building a **carcass** around itself — a shell of ice and frozen material to protect it while it feasts. The more it consumes, the stronger the shell grows.

The heart is **selective**. It doesn't want ordinary souls. It wants the children of Morð — those marked with potential. Future Berserkers, future Animists, paths not yet chosen. It feeds on what could have been.

Sválghjarta's corruption spreads through the land around it. The ice deepens. The cold intensifies. The forest whispers change. The warmth fades. The artifact plagues Nordhalla not through Morð — Morð is the Glacier Bargain's cost — but through its own insatiable hunger for potential.

### The Founder — Sylvén

Sylvén was a fisherman from **Hvalhavn**, a small northern village. He hunted with his brother Úlfrekr. They tracked wolves deep into the arctic. Sylvén took the first shot. Missed. The wolves attacked. Úlfrekr, out of bullets, was told to flee. He did.

Sylvén, broken and frostbitten, fell into a crevice. At the bottom, next to a pulsing ring of ice — Sválghjarta. The Heart whispered. It told him it would help. It told him to consume it.

He ate the ring.

His skin turned pale blue. His eyes frosted over. The hunger began. Not physical — spiritual. An emptiness that nothing could fill. He killed the wolves. He devoured them. Not for sustenance. Because the compulsion was too strong.

Úlfrekr, consumed with guilt, turned back. He found Sylvén hunched over the wolf carcasses, covered in blood. He ran to his friend, apologizing for running. Sylvén looked at his brother. The Heart whispered. *More.*

In anticipation of a hug, Úlfrekr stepped forward. Sylvén bit his neck. Broke his spine. Devoured him on the frozen plateau.

Sylvén walked back to Hvalhavn. Alone. He told the village Úlfrekr was dead — killed by wolves. He told them he fought them off, begged the gods to save his brother. Instead, the gods gave him a mission. A purpose. A hunger that would save them all.

The village mourned Úlfrekr. They believed Sylvén. He was the hero who survived. He was the one the gods chose.

**Sylvén is a false prophet.** He genuinely believes the Heart chose him. He genuinely believes the hunger is a gift. He genuinely believes the children are being transformed, not consumed. The Heart whispers to him — and the whispers lie. They tell him he's saving them. They tell him the frost is mercy. He believes it because the alternative is that he killed his brother and eats children for nothing. He's not evil. He's wrong. And he's too deep in the hunger to see it.

### The Hungríd — Hierarchy of Hunger

The cult. Not a religion. Not an army. A family of the starved. Everyone who follows Sylvén has felt the hunger. They're all connected through it. They view themselves as chosen — the starved folk who will retake their place in society and the world.

**The ranking system:**

| Rank | Title | Who | Powers | Knowledge |
|------|-------|-----|--------|-----------|
| **The Hollow King** | Sylvén | The founder. The Heart-Eater. | Rime-infused. Can freeze with a touch to the chest. The strongest. | Believes the Heart's whispers. Thinks he's a prophet. |
| **The Frostborn** | Inner circle (5-7) | Those Sylvén has marked directly. His earliest followers. | Rime-infused. Can freeze with touch. Weaker than Sylvén but strong. | Know children are consumed. Stay silent because they'd lose their powers. |
| **The Tooth** | Enforcers | Warriors who carry out captures and bounties. | Marked but not rime-infused. Stronger than normal humans. | Believe the sacrifice story. Think children are sent to the deity. |
| **The Hollow** | Scouts and watchers | Infiltrators in towns and villages. | Marked. Slightly enhanced. | Believe the sacrifice story. Spread bounties. |
| **The Starved** | Rank and file | Followers who've taken the mark. | Marked. Frost-touched skin. Can endure cold. | Believe the children walk the ice and are empowered. |

**Rime-infused powers:** The Heart grants its closest followers the ability to freeze with a touch to the chest. Not just cold — rime. The frost that forms on dead things. The cold that stops hearts. Sylvén can do it instantly. The Frostborn can do it with effort. It's the Heart's signature — the void made physical.

**The bounty system:** Hungríd clans post bounties for children of Morð. Not kill-bounties — capture bounties. The children are valuable. The higher members know they're taken to Sylvén and consumed. The lower members think they're sent to the deity. The illusion is maintained because:

1. The lower members believe the children are empowered, not eaten
2. The higher members would lose their rime-infused powers if they spoke
3. Some children ARE released — the ones the Heart doesn't want. They come back frost-touched and tell stories of "being chosen." They're living proof of the lie.

**How the cult recruits:** Sylvén's power is visible. His marked followers have frosted eyes and frostburned skin — proof of the Heart's favor. They're stronger. They don't feel cold. They can fight. In a world where Morð takes mothers and the cold kills without mercy, the Hungríd offer strength. The price is the mark. The price is the hunger. The price is serving Sylvén.

The Starving Touch creates dependence. Those Sylvén bites become dependent on the hunger. They can't eat, can't sleep, can't think about anything but the Heart's need. They follow him because they have to. Not because they want to.

### Why the Heart Wants Children of Morð

Morð selects children with potential — future Berserkers, Animists, paths not yet chosen. The Heart feeds on **potential**. The road not taken. The power never realized. It's not eating souls — it's eating **possibility**. The void inside it is filled by what could have been.

Ordinary children don't interest it. They don't have enough unrealized potential. Only the marked ones. Only the children Morð already chose.

The love bond matters. The Heart wants the warmth of authentic creation — a mother's love for her child. That bond can't be faked. The Hungríd can't just kidnap women and impregnate them — the love wouldn't be real. The Heart would reject it. It wants the genuine article. That's why they target mothers who are already pregnant, already bonded, already carrying authentic warmth.

### How the Cult Operates

**Bounties:** Posted in Hungríd settlements for children known to be of Morð. Higher members know the truth. Lower members believe the sacrifice story. Rumors spread. Most believe the sacrifice to the deity by sending the children off, but the children are valuable so others are kidnapped and sent off to have the illusion in place.

**Kidnapping:** Specific. Targeted. Children of Morð are the prize. The Hollow watches pregnancies. The Tooth captures. The children are taken to Sylvén's keep. The Heart chooses. The ones it wants are consumed. The others are released as "chosen" — living proof of the lie.

**Occupation:** The Hungríd are not just a cult. They're an occupation. Camps and strongholds across Nordhalla — logging the Whispering Pine, mining the mountains, tapping geothermal warmth, watching the coasts. They're building the Heart's carcass. They're silencing the forest's whispers. They're stealing the land's warmth.

### The Whispering Pine Corruption

The Whispering Pine is ancient. The trees whisper because they remember. The Hungríd are cutting the trees. Every tree they fell silences a whisper. The forest is being deafened. The corruption isn't supernatural — it's industrial. The Hungríd are destroying the forest's memory. The Heart benefits because a deafened forest can't warn anyone.

The Corvani feel it most. Their settlements in the Whispering Pine are being pushed out. The trees they live in are being cut. The whispers they depend on are going silent. The Corvani are refugees in their own home.

### The Volcanic Warmth

The volcanic vents, the Eldøyane, the geothermal warmth — it's not just geothermal activity. It's Sol's essence bleeding through the earth. The last remnant of the sun's warmth. Keth-Amar feeds on this warmth. The closer you get to the volcanic vents, the more you feel his influence. The whispers grow stronger. The hunger intensifies. The Pale Neth at Saltgrinn guard the Eldøyane jealously — they don't know why the warmth matters, only that it does.

A massive fortification built by Jarn-Tand to regulate the **Øsling** (renamed from Fredløse — see below) clans and enforce trade monopoly. It funnels every nomad migration through taxed checkpoints. The Ordan clans who once crossed free now pay Skalvyr tolls for the land they were born on.

## PART VII: PEOPLES

### 🧊 The Skald (Dominant Human Subrace)

The viking-like subrace of Nordhalla. Pale, weathered, hard-faced. Saga-culture with glacier-entombment tradition. The dead are set upright in the ice as permanent witnesses. There is no separate "Rime-Born" race — the Skald are the people.

**Subgroups:** Skald Keepers, Ice-Trackers, Skald Navy, Sea-Guard, Rune Keepers, Endurance Purists, Frost Chanters (Øsling), Berserkers (Rime-Touched)

### ❄️ The Rime-Touched (Status, Not a Race)

A Skald who survives Morð is **Rime-Touched** — marked by Keth-Amar's interest. They are the children the bargain collects. They are what Keth-Amar wanted from the start.

**Traits:** Blue skin, supernatural cold in blood, frost-touched hands (immune to burns), slow aging, vision issues in warmth. They work the forges of the Bloodhammer Sump. They serve as Berserkers.

**Morð:** 1 in 7 mothers die. The child takes the warmth. The mother dies. The child survives, marked. **Nothing can change the base rate.** Only a Berserker can intervene, and the Berserker dies.

### 🐦‍⬛ The Corvani (Non-Playable Subfolk) — Cursed Former Humans

A race of **cursed former humans** — highland survivors who refused to descend into the fjord-keeps, marked by an ancient hermit-spirit. They are **goblin/harpy/raven-folk** — scavengers, hoarders of shiny objects, opportunistic messengers.

**Appearance:** Raven-like. Some have feathers mixed with hair. Eyes with iridescent glint. Claws, sharp teeth. Build varies — some human-tall, some small and darting.

**Culture:** Rich and complex. They nest in **glacier-caves** and **woodland roosts**. They have a social hierarchy based on who owns the shiniest hoard. They are scavengers, tinkerers, traders. They work as messengers because it gets them out of the keep-walls. They love shiny objects — coins, glass, ice-crystals, mirrors. They collect them.

**Language:** **Corvid-Speech** — polyphonic tongue combining soft clicks, whistling, low-frequency throat vibrations, and subtle marking-shifts.

**Settlements:** Vespera's Perch (glacier-caves), Ravencall Eyrie (woodland roosts), plus unnumbered glacier and woodland nests throughout the Snowcall mountains and forests.

### 🐦‍⬛ THE CORVANI CURSE — The Hermit of the High Peaks

#### The Highland Raiders

In the pre-Binding era, the highland survivors of Nordhalla were already a **bandity, scavenger people**. They lived in the glacier-spires and high pine forests, refusing the lowland Skald kings' tribute demands. They survived by raiding — taking from those weaker, smaller, or more alone. The hermit was not their first victim. He would not be their last.

#### The Robbery

One winter, during a famine, a band of highlanders raided the camp of a **withered hermit** who lived alone in a glacier-crevice. The hermit was ancient — so old his kind had been forgotten. They took his winter stores, his fire-wood, his salt. They thought him mad, helpless, a target. They left him to die in the cold.

He didn't die.

#### The Curse

When spring came, the hermit climbed down from his crevice and walked through every highland camp. He spoke a curse in a language no one had ever heard — a tongue of clicks, whistles, and throat-vibrations that the wind had been teaching him for a hundred winters. The curse **bound them to the shape of the ravens** — to scavenging, hoarding, nesting, opportunistic taking. They would never again be fully human. They would become the very embodiment of what they had done to him.

The highlanders tried to fight it. Some succeeded in descending to the lowland keeps, becoming "ordinary" Skald or Vaegfolk. But those who stayed in the highlands, who refused to give up their isolation, found their bodies slowly **changing**. Feathers in the hair. Claws on the fingers. Eyes that caught the light like a raven's. Voices that cracked into polyphonic whistles.

#### The Hermit Still Lives

The hermit is **still out there**, somewhere in the highest crevices of the Icetalon Peaks. The Corvani have never found him. They have looked. They will keep looking. He is an **ancient spirit** who lives in solitude for reasons no one knows — perhaps the curse was the last thing he had to do, and now he is simply waiting.

#### The Sacrifice Ritual

The Corvani have developed a **ritual of appeasement**. They gather shiny objects — coins, glass, ice-crystals, mirrors, fragments of metal, anything that glints — and carry them to the highest peaks they can reach. They leave the offerings in cairns, in crevices, on ledges. **Sometimes the objects are gone the next day.** Sometimes they sit for years. The Corvani don't know which.

They believe the sacrifices will one day **revert their transformation**. They believe that if they give enough, if they find the right offering, the hermit will lift the curse and they will be human again. They have believed this for eight centuries.

#### The Truth They Cannot See

The curse was a **mirror**. The hermit made them the embodiment of what they had done to him — scavengers, hoarders, taker-of-shiny-things-from-the-alone. **If they could reject their nature** — the bandity, the scavenging, the hoarding — **they would slowly transform back.** The path to redemption is the rejection of what they are.

**But they cannot reject it.** It is their nature now, the curse and the truth of it are the same. They will always be scavengers. They will always gather shiny things. They will always carry them to peaks in hope. The ritual is itself the curse made manifest.

The redemption is locked inside the lock. The key is the lock itself.

#### The Mothers' Mystery

**Corvani mothers have visibly fewer raven traits than the males.** Their feathers are sparser, their claws shorter, their voices less polyphonic. The Corvani have long attributed this to the appeasement ritual — the females "sacrifice" less because they don't go to the peaks as often, so the curse is satisfied.

The truth is simpler and more damning: **the mothers don't scavenge as much.** The curse manifests through action — every time a Corvani takes something that wasn't freely given, hoards a shiny thing, or raids a weaker creature, the curse takes deeper root. The females, who stay closer to the nest, who raise the young, who trade and bargain rather than raid, simply **do less of what the curse feeds on.** The curse is starved in them. They transform less because they live less of the curse's appetite.

**The Corvani think the ritual is helping.** It isn't. The ritual is itself the curse. The mothers are proof of what the Corvani cannot see — that redemption is in the rejection of their nature, not in appeasing the hermit who cursed them.

### ⚔️ The Øsling Clans (formerly Fredløse)

The "lawless" of Nordhalla. Highland survivors who refused the bargain or were exiled for it. They live beyond the Sunder-Wall in the frozen wastes no Skalvyr tax-collector reaches. They raid, mammoth-hunt, and keep the old songs the Runic Academies want burned.

**Frost Chanters** preserve pre-Bargain oral history. A Skald who walks beyond the Sunder-Wall can become Øsling in a single winter. An Øsling who walks back can never quite return — the songs do not wash out of the throat.

Rime-Touched smiths. Work the **Bloodhammer Sump** forges. Frost-touched hands are immune to burns. They forge runic cold-iron axes. Their ancestor Torra led the clans south through the Cragjaw to Emberspire during a brief expedition. The Berserker tradition's primary source.

### 👽 The Astril in Nordhalla

The Astril are an alien race primarily in Sundrift Vale. They have a **small scholar-community in Frostholm** and near the **Frozen Archive** — they remember the bargain, they know who the houses thought they were contacting versus who actually answered. They are eccentric, soft-spoken, and **never speak of Keth-Amar by name in public**. They have a scholar-quarter in the capital.

**Why they're in Nordhalla:** They have a research interest in the binding and the bargain. Some of their number feel responsible for not warning the Skald. They are a small, quiet community — easily missed in the bustle of Frostholm.

### 📜 The High Neth in Snowcall (Diaspora Community)

The Neth are a race from **Bryngloom Forest** (their capital home), where they live in the cathedral-grove of Atropolis. They are pale-skinned, formal, document-oriented, tied to contract-magic and the Sovereign Ledger tradition. The Arcanoneer class is Neth-native.

The **High Neth** are Neth immigrants in Nordhalla — a small community centered in **Snowcall City**, with a small presence in Frostholm. They've been in Nordhalla for several generations, brought by trade, by the Syndicate's contracts, and by their own curiosity about the Morð records.

**The "High Neth" sub-designation** refers to Neth who settled in the highland/mountain valleys of Snowcall. Not a separate biological subrace — they are Neth adapted by circumstance:
- Paler than lowland Neth (less sun, more snow-reflection)
- Slightly frost-touched (some intermarried with Skald over generations)
- Formally dressed in layered black wool, always with a quill or contract-scroll
- Speak Neth-tongue at home, Common with the Skald, Old Nord for elders
- Tend to live in the upper floors of buildings, near steam vents
- Particular fondness for **storing records in ice** — the Frozen Archive's runic preservation methods fascinate them

**What they do:**
- **Archive work** — alongside Skald Keepers in the Frozen Archive
- **Contract law** — drafting the Icechamber Syndicate's trade agreements
- **Arcanoneer practice** — small contract-chamber in Snowcall City
- **Diplomacy with Bryngloom** — correspondence with Atropolis

**Neth presence in Nordhalla:**

## PART XII: THE SORTE MARKET (Frigga's Compact)

**Replaces the old Silence-Heat Heresy.**

Within living memory, **Frigga Skalvyr** led a small group of nobles who wanted a solution to the cold. They couldn't reach the Sky-Court or call on dead gods, so they went to the black markets on the open seas.

There, they bought **Clockwork Fexric and Caustic Fexric** from slave-auction houses — illegally immigrating them to Nordhalla through hidden southern coves at Xardin's Hearth. The fexric have skills the Skald lack: clockwork mechanisms that work in cold, alchemical concoctions that can warm stone.

The fexric were put to work in **sweatshops** beneath Frostholm and the Frozen Archive. The capable ones built mechanisms and brewed concoctions. The less-capable became household servants — and the source of the Skald fexric diaspora in the cities.

This is the dirty secret. The heresy isn't the heat-engine — it's the **slave trade**. Frigga brokered the deal. The Skald houses benefit from fexric labor every day. The Øsling know. The Astril know. The High Neth audit but turn a blind eye. No one speaks of it openly.

**Why no Pyrofiend involvement:** The Pyrofiend class was "made" when the deals were set and Sundale's volcano exploded (along with other minor volcanoes worldwide). The Sorte Market pre-dates the Pyrofiend founding. Frigga didn't have access to Pyrofiend fire yet.

**Entry point:** Xardin's Hearth harbor-district's back-alleys. The "Sorte Market" is the local name for the operation.

## PART XIII: KEY CONFLICTS & EVENTS

### The Glacier Bargain (Binding Era)
The Skaldic houses thought they were bargaining with a benevolent frost deity. Keth-Amar (disguised) accepted. He halted the glaciers. The real price: the heirs of the binding houses, key to breaking Aex. Morð is the mechanism that delivers them.

### The Astril's Complicity
The Astril helped the houses make contact. They had their own sun devoured by Keth-Amar. They didn't warn the Skald. The Nordhalla Astril carry this guilt.

### The Hunger Pact's Replacement — Berserker Sacrifice
The Berserker class is the answer to Morð. The Rime-Touched can learn to channel Morð into themselves through Blóð, sparing the mother at the cost of their own flame. The Cradle-Camp is staffed by Berserkers waiting to die.

### The Corvani Curse (Pre-Binding)
Highland raiders robbed a hermit. The hermit cursed them to become the embodiment of what they did — scavengers, hoarders, taker-of-shiny-things. The Corvani's appeasement ritual is itself the curse made manifest. The redemption is locked inside the lock.

### Torra's Migration (Early Dimming)
Torra Bloodhammer led the clans south through the Cragjaw to Emberspire. A third died. Grum ignited the first Blood-Heat. Torra died 3 days after arriving.

### The Sorte Market (Recent)
Frigga brokered the fexric slave-trade. Fexric sweatshops beneath Frostholm and the Archive. The hidden shame of House Skalvyr.

### The Cleansing of the Hearth (Ongoing)
House Skalvyr's campaign against tribal Animists. Runic Academies suppress ancestral traditions.

### Morð (Ongoing)
1 in 7 mothers die. Always. Berserkers are stationed at the Cradle-Camp, ready to make the ultimate sacrifice.

### The Doom-Count (Current)
Malakor calculated the end. The Doom-Choir sings the count from the Frozen Archive.

### The False Prophet Plot (Current)
Mor-Vereth's Congregation of the Silence is receiving specific instructions from the Voice — all pointing to the Frozen Archive's lowest vault. Something trapped there may be arranging its release.
